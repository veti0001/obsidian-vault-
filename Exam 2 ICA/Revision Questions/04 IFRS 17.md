# IFRS 17 — Revision Questions

## Part A — Definitions and Recall

Source: [[Contract Boundaries]], [[CSM]], [[Discount Rate]], [[Estimates of Future Cash Flows]], [[Level of Aggregation]], [[LRC]], [[PAA Eligibility]], [[Premium Allocation Approach (PAA)]], [[RA]], [[Reinsurance Contract]], [[Unit of Account]]

### A1. Contract boundary principle
Define when cash flows arise within the contract boundary of an insurance contract.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Cash flows arise within the contract boundary when they result from substantive rights and obligations that exist during periods in which the entity can compel the policyholder to pay premiums, or the entity has a substantive obligation to provide insurance contract services.

### A2. Individual-level reassessment
When does the substantive obligation end at the individual policyholder level?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The substantive obligation ends when the entity has the practical ability to reassess the risks of the particular policyholder and can set a price or benefit level that fully reflects those risks.

### A3. Portfolio-level reassessment conditions
State the two conditions that must both be satisfied for the substantive obligation to end at the portfolio level.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The entity has the practical ability to reassess the risks of the portfolio containing the contract and can set a price or benefit level that fully reflects the risk of that portfolio; and
> - Premiums priced up to the reassessment date do not take into account risks relating to periods after the reassessment date.

### A4. CSM definition
Define the Contractual Service Margin (CSM) under IFRS 17.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - A component of the carrying amount of the asset or liability for a group of insurance contracts representing the unearned profit the entity will recognize as it provides insurance contract services under the insurance contracts in the group.

### A5. CSM recognition approach
How is CSM recognized in profit or loss over time?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - CSM is recognized gradually in line with the insurance contract services provided, using coverage units to determine the amount recognized in each reporting period.

### A6. CSM under reinsurance held
How does CSM behave for a reinsurance contract held?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - An onerous reinsurance held contract does not exist; the CSM can be negative (representing the net cost of purchasing reinsurance).

### A7. Coverage units: policy amount principle
In determining coverage units, is the quantity of benefits based on expected claims or on the amount that can be claimed by the policyholder?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The quantity of benefits is related to the amount that can be claimed by the policyholder, not the expected claims or the release of the risk adjustment.

### A8. Discount rate: illiquidity premium
Define the illiquidity premium in the context of discount rates.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - An adjustment made to a liquid risk-free yield curve to reflect differences between the liquidity characteristics of the financial instruments underlying the risk-free rates observed in the market and the liquidity characteristics of the insurance contracts.

### A9. Bottom-up discount rate formula
State the bottom-up formula for the discount rate.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Bottom-up discount rate = Risk-free rate + Illiquidity premium.

### A10. Top-down discount rate formula
State the top-down formula for the discount rate.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Top-down discount rate = Reference portfolio rate minus credit risk, market risk and other relevant adjustments.

### A11. Estimates of future cash flows: characteristics
List four key characteristics required for estimates of future cash flows under IFRS 17.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Include all future cash flows within the contract boundary;
> - Are estimates of the probability-weighted mean of the full range of possible outcomes;
> - Are unbiased (do not include the risk adjustment for non-financial risk);
> - Reflect the entity perspective (with market variables consistent with observable market values) and are current and explicit.

### A12. Unbiased cash flows
What does “unbiased” mean in the context of estimating future cash flows?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - It means capturing the full range of possible outcomes without an intention to attain a particular outcome or to influence a particular behavior; there is no presumption of conservatism.

### A13. Level of aggregation conditions
Identify the two portfolio conditions and one key grouping constraint at initial recognition.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Contracts in a portfolio are managed together and subject to similar risks;
> - Contracts are grouped based on whether they are onerous/non-onerous at initial recognition and contracts issued more than one year apart cannot be included in the same group;
> - PAA and GMA contracts must be separated.

### A14. LRC definition elements
Briefly describe the two broad elements of the Liability for Remaining Coverage (LRC).

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The obligation for insured events that have not yet occurred (unexpired portion of insurance coverage); and
> - Amounts for insurance contract services not yet provided (and any investment components/other amounts not yet transferred to LIC).

### A15. Unit of account recognition triggers
State the three dates on which an insurance contract can be first recognized.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The beginning of the coverage period;
> - The date when the first payment from the policyholder is due;
> - The date when the group becomes onerous.

### A16. PAA eligibility (core)
List the two main eligibility criteria for using the Premium Allocation Approach (PAA).

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Reasonably expect that the simplification would produce a measurement of LRC for the group that would not differ materially from GMA (at inception and at each future reporting period within the coverage period); or alternatively the coverage period of each contract is one year or less;
> - Also consider expected significant variability in fulfilment cash flows (if significant it may cause material difference).

### A17. RA definition
Define the Risk Adjustment for non-financial risk (RA).

> [!answer]- Answer
> - An adjustment to the estimate of the present value of future cash flows to reflect the compensation the entity requires for bearing the uncertainty about the amount and timing of cash flows arising from non-financial risk.

### A18. Reinsurance contract held: level of aggregation
What is a notable feature about the level of aggregation for reinsurance contracts held compared with insurance contracts issued?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The level of aggregation (portfolios and groups) may differ from the underlying insurance/reinsurance contracts issued; it is reasonable for a portfolio of reinsurance contracts to contain a single contract.

### A19. Reinsurance held: CSM sign
Can the CSM on a reinsurance contract held be negative?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Yes; unlike CSM for underlying insurance contracts, CSM on reinsurance contracts held can be positive or negative.

### A20. Disclosure: RA required items
Name two disclosures required in relation to the Risk Adjustment.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The confidence level of the reported RA and the approach used to determine the RA (and also a reconciliation of opening/closing RA and significant judgments/changes).

## Part B — Explain, Compare, Advantages and Disadvantages

Source: [[Contract Boundaries]], [[CSM]], [[Discount Rate]], [[Estimates of Future Cash Flows]], [[LRC]], [[PAA Eligibility]], [[Premium Allocation Approach (PAA)]], [[RA]], [[Reinsurance Contract]]

### B1. Explain contract boundary
Explain the difference between individual-level and portfolio-level reassessment in determining the contract boundary.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Individual-level: obligation ends when entity can reassess particular policyholder risk and set price/benefit fully reflecting that risk.
> - Portfolio-level: obligation ends only when entity can reassess portfolio risk and set price/benefit fully reflecting portfolio risk, and premiums priced up to reassessment date do not take into account risks after reassessment date.

### B2. Explain CSM purpose
Explain why IFRS 17 introduces the CSM and how it affects profit recognition.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - CSM represents unearned profit to be recognized as insurance contract services are provided; profit is recognized over time (not at initial recognition) for non-onerous groups; if onerous, CSM is zero and loss component applies.

### B3. Explain coverage units
Explain the principles used to determine coverage units for amortizing CSM, including discounting and coverage period.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Quantity of benefits relates to amount claimable by policyholder (not expected claims or RA release); reflect different service levels across periods and expected contract duration including lapses/cancellations; discounting in quantity of benefits is optional and if used must be applied consistently; coverage period extends to end of period insurance services provided, not to settlement period (unless claims in settlement in LRC rather than LIC).

### B4. Compare bottom-up vs top-down discount rate
Compare bottom-up and top-down approaches for determining discount rates, giving one advantage and one disadvantage of each.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Bottom-up: risk-free curve plus illiquidity premium. Advantage: availability of risk-free yield curves. Disadvantage: need to derive illiquidity premium.
> - Top-down: reference portfolio yield minus credit/market/other adjustments. Advantage: no explicit illiquidity premium derivation. Disadvantage: complexity in deriving reference portfolio rate and adjustments.

### B5. Explain liquidity characteristics of LRC vs LIC
Explain why the LRC is generally liquid and the LIC generally illiquid under IFRS 17.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - LRC generally liquid because policyholders can cancel policies before expiry and receive value without significant exit costs (reinsurance held assessed on purchaser’s ability to cancel and receive value).
> - LIC generally illiquid because policyholders typically cannot obtain exit value in advance of normal payment dates.

### B6. Explain unbiased estimates
Explain what makes estimates of future cash flows “unbiased” and why that matters.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Unbiased means full range of possible outcomes captured, no intention to attain a particular outcome, no intention to influence behavior, and no presumption of conservatism; this supports a probability-weighted mean that reflects the entity’s current view without RA.

### B7. Explain PAA vs GMA usefulness
Explain the usefulness of the PAA compared to the GMA for LRC measurement.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - PAA provides simplification for LRC when eligible (no need to calculate fulfilment cash flows or amortize CSM for non-onerous groups in the same way); LIC measurement is similar to GMA; choice considers eligibility extent, systems/support, and additional resources/costs of GMA.

### B8. Explain RA diversification
Explain how diversification affects the RA and how benefits might be reflected in aggregate versus unit-of-account approaches.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Diversification can reduce RA for non-financial risk; in aggregate approach diversification benefits reflected in the aggregate risk distribution via correlation/copulas/judgment; in unit-of-account approach RA for a unit may not reflect all entity diversification, so confidence in RA may be higher (more conservative).

### B9. Explain reinsurance held FCF adjustments
Explain the treatment of risk of non-performance by the reinsurer in fulfilment cash flows for reinsurance contracts held.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The effect of any risk of non-performance by the issuer of the reinsurance contract is included as a decrease to the estimates of future cash inflows; CSM is not adjusted for that change, but the asset/liability (ARC) is adjusted via changes in FCF.

### B10. Compare RA calculation methods
Compare the Quantile method and the Cost of Capital method for RA, giving one advantage and one disadvantage of each.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Quantile (VaR/CTE): advantage gives direct confidence level; disadvantage can introduce spurious accuracy if distribution/method misused.
> - Cost of Capital: advantage conceptually close to RA definition and supports granular allocation; disadvantage operationally complex.

## Part C — Application and Calculation

Source: [[CSM]], [[Discount Rate]], [[Estimates of Future Cash Flows]], [[LRC]], [[PAA Eligibility]], [[Premium Allocation Approach (PAA)]], [[RA]]

### C1. CSM at initial recognition
At initial recognition, a group of non-onerous insurance contracts has the following (amounts in CU):
- Present value of fulfilment cash outflows: $1,200,000$
- Present value of fulfilment cash inflows (premiums): $1,400,000$
- Risk adjustment for non-financial risk: $50,000$

Calculate the CSM at initial recognition.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Total fulfilment cash flows = $-1,200,000 - 50,000 + 1,400,000 = 150,000$ (net inflow in fulfilment terms) so CSM = $150,000$.

### C2. CSM release using coverage units
A group has CSM at start of period of $300,000$. Coverage units expected for remaining period = $1,000,000$. Coverage units provided in current period = $150,000$. 
Calculate CSM recognized in profit or loss for the period (release amount).

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Release fraction = $150,000 / 1,000,000 = 0.15$
> - CSM recognized = $300,000 \times 0.15 = 45,000$.

### C3. Onerous contract test
Group cash flows (CU):
- PV of cash inflows: $800,000$
- PV of cash outflows (excluding RA): $850,000$
- RA: $30,000$

Is the group onerous? Show calculation and conclusion.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Net fulfilment = $800,000 - 850,000 - 30,000 = -80,000$
> - Yes, onerous (net outflow) so no CSM; loss component = $80,000$.

### C4. Bottom-up discount rate
Risk-free yield curve rate for relevant term = $3.00\%$. Estimated illiquidity premium = $0.60\%$.
Compute the bottom-up discount rate.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - $3.00\% + 0.60\% = 3.60\%$.

### C5. Top-down discount rate
Reference portfolio yield = $5.20\%$. Adjustments for credit risk, market risk, and other factors = $1.40\%$.
Compute top-down discount rate.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - $5.20\% - 1.40\% = 3.80\%$.

### C6. Present value of cash flows
Expected cash outflow in year 3 = $500,000$. Discount rate = $4.00\%$ per annum (discrete). Calculate PV at time 0.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - $PV = 500,000 / (1.04)^3 \approx 500,000 / 1.124864 = 444,498$.

### C7. LRC under PAA (non-onerous)
For a non-onerous group under PAA: total premiums received = $1,200,000$, insurance revenue recognized for services provided = $900,000$, unexpired acquisition costs deferred = $60,000$, financing adjustment = $-10,000$. 
Calculate LRC for remaining coverage.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - LRC = (Prem received - Revenue recognized) - Acquisition costs deferred + Financing adjustment = $(1,200,000 - 900,000) - 60,000 + (-10,000) = 300,000 - 60,000 - 10,000 = 230,000$.

### C8. RA quantile (VaR)
Probability-weighted mean PV of cash flows = $2,000,000$. Value-at-Risk at 90% confidence level for PV of cash flows = $2,300,000$.
Calculate RA under quantile method.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - $RA = VaR(90\%) - Mean = 2,300,000 - 2,000,000 = 300,000$.

### C9. RA CTE
Mean PV = $1,500,000$. Conditional Tail Expectation at 80% for the tail = $1,700,000$.
Calculate RA.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - $RA = CTE(80\%) - Mean = 1,700,000 - 1,500,000 = 200,000$.

### C10. Reinsurance held: ceded effect
Gross RA = $400,000$. Net RA (reflecting reinsurance held) = $250,000$. 
State the ceded RA amount (asset effect conceptually).

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Ceded RA = $400,000 - 250,000 = 150,000$ (reported as a positive asset for reinsurance held).

### C11. PAA eligibility materiality test
Expected LRC under PAA at end of period 1 = $850,000$. Expected LRC under GMA at same date = $870,000$. Materiality threshold = $30,000$. 
Would this difference be considered material? Support with calculation.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Difference = $|870,000 - 850,000| = 20,000$
> - $20,000 < 30,000$ so difference not material; supports PAA eligibility on this test.

### C12. Coverage period contract boundary
A contract allows repricing of all risks within 12 months with no restrictions. What is the typical contract boundary length and implication for PAA?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Contract boundary typically one year or less; this satisfies the coverage period criterion for automatic PAA eligibility consideration (subject to materiality/significant variability).

### C13. CSM amortization pattern
Identify amortization pattern for: (a) same policy limit throughout coverage; (b) decreasing policy limit over coverage; (c) increasing policy limit over coverage.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - (a) Uniform
> - (b) Declining
> - (c) Increasing

### C14. Reinsurance held recognition timing
When is a group of reinsurance contracts held recognized (earliest of two dates)?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Beginning of coverage period of the group of reinsurance contracts held; or date the entity recognizes an onerous group of underlying insurance contracts, if the related reinsurance contract was entered into at or before that date.

### C15. Risk of non-performance
How is the risk of non-performance by the reinsurer reflected in fulfilment cash flows for reinsurance held?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Included as a decrease to the estimates of future cash inflows.

### C16. Discount rate selection factors
Name two factors relevant when selecting adjustments in the top-down approach to eliminate non-relevant factors.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Credit risk (default/downgrade) and market risk; also consistency of timing of payments between reference portfolio assets and insurance liabilities and currency alignment.

## Part D — Exam-Style Questions

Source: [[Contract Boundaries]], [[CSM]], [[Discount Rate]], [[Estimates of Future Cash Flows]], [[Level of Aggregation]], [[LRC]], [[PAA Eligibility]], [[RA]], [[Reinsurance Contract]]

### D1. Contract boundary rationale
“Contract boundaries under IFRS 17 are driven by substantive rights and obligations, not simply legal form or stated contract term.” 
Discuss this statement, covering both individual and portfolio reassessment and practical implications for determining boundary dates.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Focus on substantive ability: boundary determined when entity can compel premium or has substantive obligation to provide services.
> - Individual reassessment: practical ability to reassess particular policyholder risk and set price/benefit fully reflecting it ends obligation.
> - Portfolio reassessment: requires practical ability to reassess portfolio risk and set price/benefit fully reflecting portfolio risk, plus premiums up to reassessment date do not include post-date risks.
> - Practical implications: boundaries may differ from legal term; repricing rights/constraints and how premiums are priced matter; boundary affects which cash flows are included in measurement.

### D2. CSM vs profit recognition
Explain why the CSM exists, how it is determined at initial recognition, and how it is amortized. Include the impact of onerous contracts and the treatment for reinsurance held.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Purpose: unearned profit recognized as services provided over time (avoids front-loading).
> - Initial recognition: CSM = net of fulfilment cash flows for non-onerous group (inflows minus outflows including RA) — if net outflow, group onerous with loss component.
> - Amortization: based on coverage units reflecting quantity of benefits claimable, service levels, expected duration/lapses; discounting optional if applied consistently; coverage period ends at service provision (not settlement).
> - Onerous: no CSM, loss component replaces.
> - Reinsurance held: onerous reinsurance held does not exist and CSM can be negative (net cost of purchase).

### D3. Discount rate methodology choice
An insurer is considering bottom-up, top-down, or hybrid approaches for discount rates. Discuss advantages, disadvantages, and practical considerations in selecting an approach.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Bottom-up: + available risk-free curves; − need illiquidity premium derivation.
> - Top-down: + no explicit illiquidity premium; − complexity of reference portfolio and adjustments (credit/market/timing/currency).
> - Hybrid: blends robust illiquidity premium from reference portfolio with risk-free curve.
> - Considerations: data availability, liquidity characteristics of contracts (LRC more liquid, LIC less liquid), currency, consistency, systems, and ability to support required disclosures.

### D4. PAA eligibility and loss component
Explain the criteria for PAA eligibility, how significant variability and materiality are assessed, and the implications when a group becomes onerous.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Criteria: coverage period <=1 year for each contract OR reasonable expectation that PAA LRC not materially different from GMA at inception and each future reporting date; also consider significant variability.
> - Assessment: compare expected LRC under PAA vs GMA across reporting dates within coverage period; materiality thresholds applied; qualitative assessment may suffice in obvious cases; variability significant if it causes material difference.
> - Onerous: if group onerous, entity must calculate GMA LRC estimate to determine loss component (even if eligible for PAA, onerous test requires that); PAA can still apply to LIC in some cases but loss component measurement requires GMA basis for onerousness assessment.

### D5. RA: methods and reinsurance
Discuss the main methods for calculating RA for non-financial risk, factors influencing choice, and how RA is treated for reinsurance contracts held.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Methods: Quantile (VaR/CTE) — direct confidence level but needs distribution; Cost of Capital — conceptually aligned and supports allocation but complex; Margin method — simple but confidence level harder.
> - Choice influenced by data, skewness, correlation, diversification, systems, and desire for confidence level disclosure.
> - Reinsurance held: RA represents risk ceded to reinsurer (asset effect); can estimate via difference (gross-net), ceded data, proportional scaling in some cases, or CoC; risk of non-performance decreases future cash inflows.

### D6. Level of aggregation and contract recognition
Explain level of aggregation requirements, why grouping constraints exist, and when insurance contracts are first recognized, including reinsurance held timing.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Portfolio: managed together and subject to similar risks. Groups: onerous/non-onerous; no contracts issued >1 year apart in same group; PAA and GMA separated.
> - Constraints prevent offsetting onerous with non-onerous and ensure appropriate measurement.
> - Recognition (issued): beginning of coverage period, or first premium due date, or date group becomes onerous (earliest).
> - Reinsurance held recognition: beginning of coverage period of reinsurance group, or date onerous underlying group recognized if reinsurance entered into at or before that date.
