# ICA Standards of Practice — Reporting and Valuation (1700, 2200, 2400) — Revision Questions

## Part A — Definitions and Recall

Source: [[Summary report]], [[Reporting oral report]], [[Reporting internal user report]], [[Reporting external user report]], [[General]], [[Reporting]], [[Accepting and continuing an engagement]], [[Communication with the auditor]], [[Report on matters requiring rectification]], [[Report to the directors]]

### A1. Under SOP 1740, when should the actuary prepare a summary report, and what controls the wording used in it?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 1740 (Definition): "Where required by practice-specific standards, the actuary should prepare a summary report."
> - The practice-specific standards specify the language to be used in the summary report.
> - The purpose of the summary report is to simplify the actuary's communication with users.

### A2. Under SOP 1740, where can a summary report be incorporated, and what is its status relative to an external user report?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - It may be incorporated in a report prepared by the actuary's employer or client.
> - Examples given: the financial statements of an insurer, a pension plan, or a public personal injury compensation plan.
> - Such a summary report does **not** constitute an external user report.

### A3. Under SOP 1730, which two elements of a written report do not apply to an oral report? What is the chief disadvantage of oral reporting, and what is the good practice remedy?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 1730.02: except for **signature** and **report date**, the standards are the same for both oral and written reports; the requirements for an external user report or an internal user report apply equally to oral reports.
> - Disadvantage: the actuary and the user may have differing recollections of what was reported.
> - Good practice: confirm an oral report in writing, especially when there is an external user, or record it in documentation.
> - Framing: oral reporting, especially to an internal user, is both useful and inevitable in some situations.

### A4. List the four items that, at a minimum, must be disclosed in an internal user report.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 1720.04 — disclose at a minimum:
> - Any assumption or method that is new or different from the assumption of continuance of the status quo, and the rationale for the change.
> - Any reservation.
> - Any deviation from accepted actuarial practice.
> - The actuary's identity.

### A5. Under SOP 1710, what closing formalities apply to an external user report, and how does the actuary usually identify themselves for work in Canada?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 1710: the actuary should identify himself or herself and sign the report, and date the report.
> - 1710.35: for work in Canada, the actuary usually identifies as "Fellow, Canadian Institute of Actuaries" (or "FCIA" if that designation is recognized), especially when Fellowship is required or expected.
> - 1710.37: the report date is usually when the work is substantially completed — peer review, typing, photocopying and documentation may still remain.
> - 1710.38/39: sign and deliver as soon as practical; issue within a reasonable time given the terms of engagement and the needs of users.

### A6. Unless an external user report says otherwise, what is the user entitled to assume?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 1710.28 — the user is entitled to assume:
> - The work accords with accepted actuarial practice and no reservation is required.
> - The data are sufficient and reliable.
> - For periodic reports, the method is the same and the assumptions are consistent with the prior report.

### A7. State the two carve-outs SOP 2210 makes in relation to Part 1000 and to the provision for adverse deviations when work is to be in accordance with IFRS 17.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 2210.03: notwithstanding the general applicability of Part 1000, the discount rate paragraphs **1620.35–1620.37 do NOT apply** to the valuation of insurance contracts and other obligations where that valuation is to be in accordance with IFRS 17.
> - 2210.04: the IFRS 17 **risk adjustment for non-financial risk is NOT a provision for adverse deviations** as defined in paragraph 1120.55.
> - 2210.02: the Standards of Practice are intended to supplement — not replace or restate — the requirements of IFRS 17.
> - 2210 (Definition): the actuary should be familiar with IFRS 17 and apply its requirements where valuation is to be in accordance with IFRS 17 (effective January 1, 2023).

### A8. Under SOP 2230.02, what should the actuary's report contain if the actuary can report without reservation? What changes if the actuary cannot?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - If the actuary can report without reservation, the report should consist of a **scope paragraph** (describing the actuary's work) and an **opinion paragraph** (a favourable opinion on the valuation and its presentation).
> - Otherwise, the actuary should modify the standard reporting language to report **with reservation**.
> - Square-bracketed wording is variable and may be adjusted for interim statements and terminology.

### A9. Under SOP 2440, what exactly must the appointed actuary do when a matter that requires rectification is identified, and who receives the report?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The AA should identify and monitor matters that may threaten the insurer's financial condition.
> - The AA should **investigate** and then **report, as required by law**, any such matter that requires rectification to **senior management**.
> - In the case of a **Canadian insurer**, send a **copy of the report to the directors**.
> - Depending on the jurisdiction, the law may also require the report be provided to the insurer's **regulator**.
> - The report may include recommendations for rectification and should specify a **deadline** for rectification, which the AA may later extend if appropriate.
> - If there is no suitable rectification by that deadline or its extension, the AA should report the matter to the insurer's **regulator**.

### A10. According to SOP 2460, in what two circumstances is communication with the insurer's auditor desirable, and is this communication mandatory?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 2460 (Definition): communication with the insurer's auditor **would be desirable** when:
> - The actuary makes a report to the insurer's senior management on a matter requiring rectification; or
> - The actuary makes an unfavourable report on the insurer's financial condition.
> - This standard has only one paragraph (.01) and the communication is **encouraged, not mandatory** — "would be desirable".
> - Continual communication with the auditor in accordance with the CIA/CICA Joint Policy Statement is part of the process to identify and assure timely receipt of information under 2430.06.
> - "Senior management" (2410) for a Canadian insurer means the CEO, the CFO and the chief risk officer; for a foreign insurer, both the chief agent for Canada and the person designated by the insurer as having responsibility for its Canadian operation.

### A11. Under SOP 2450, what must the appointed actuary of a Canadian insurer report at least yearly to the directors, and what extra items are required by law?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Report at least yearly to the directors, **or to their audit committee if the directors so delegate**:
> - On the insurer's financial position and financial condition.
> - If required by law:
> - If the insurer has one or more **participating accounts** — on the method of allocation of income and expenses to each such participating account, and on the management of the participating account(s), the dividend policy, and the dividend scales for the participating policy owners.
> - If the insurer has **adjustable policies** in force — on the criteria established or amended by the directors for changes made by the company to the premium or charge for insurance, amount of insurance, or surrender value in respect of its adjustable policies.

### A12. Under SOP 2430, what information does the appointed actuary need for the work, and how is timely receipt of that information assured?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Information comprises the records, accounts, documents and oral briefings that provide an understanding of the insurer's operations, its obligations, and the resources available to meet them. This includes, but is not limited to:
> - Files of in-force policies and outstanding claims, including their reinsurance.
> - Policy provisions and other communications with policy owners.
> - Past experience data and past financial data.
> - Communications with auditors and regulators.
> - Pricing, underwriting and accounting practice.
> - Claims settlement practice (including case estimate practice) and cost.
> - Asset-liability management and capital management practice.
> - Enterprise risk management policy.
> - Own risk and solvency assessment (ORSA) report.
> - The **process** to identify and assure timely receipt includes an understanding of the insurer's decision-making, continual communication with members of management who can supply information, and continual communication with the auditor in accordance with the CIA/CICA Joint Policy Statement.
> - 2430 (Definition): Section 1300 applies rigorously to the engagement, and Part 1000 applies to any work within the scope of Section 2400 (2420.01); "senior management" and "directors" are defined in 2410.

## Part B — Explain, Compare, Advantages and Disadvantages

Source: [[Reporting external user report]], [[Reporting internal user report]], [[Reporting oral report]], [[Summary report]], [[Reporting]], [[Report to the directors]], [[Accepting and continuing an engagement]]

### B1. Compare the range of appropriate reports for external user reports versus internal user reports.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Internal user reports (1720.02):** the range is *wider*. At one end, a formal internal user report may differ little from an external user report; at the other, an informal, abbreviated, even oral, report may suffice for a representative of the actuary's employer or client who is well-versed in the subject and communicates frequently with the actuary.
> - **External user reports (1710.06):** the range of appropriate reports is *relatively narrow* by comparison; be relatively formal and detailed when the actuary does not communicate directly with users, or when the interests of the external user and of the client/employer are not the same.
> - **Abbreviating is efficient** for both actuary and user (1720.02), provided that complete and clear communication is not thereby compromised.
> - **How much to abbreviate (1720.03):** consider the internal user's familiarity with the subject and the circumstances affecting the work; in general a **lesser** degree of abbreviation is expected when the internal user is the actuary's **client**.
> - The internal user report is explicitly permitted to abbreviate the recommendations for external user reports.

### B2. Compare the content requirements of an external user report with the minimum disclosures required for an internal user report.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **External user report (1710)** is a long list: identify the client or employer; describe the work, its purpose and its users; say that use may not be suitable for another purpose; state whether the work accords with accepted actuarial practice in Canada and disclose any deviation; disclose any unusual application of accepted actuarial practice; disclose model limitations relevant to the intended purpose; disclose any aspect of the work for which the actuary does not take responsibility; describe each material assumption and the extent of any margin for adverse deviations; provide the rationale for each such assumption; disclose any non-model/non-data assumption differing from the status quo and, if practical, useful and appropriate, the effect of alternatives; describe the methods; in a periodic report disclose inconsistencies with prior reports and the rationale; describe subsequent events not taken into account; disclose any reservation; express an opinion on the assumptions and methods; express an opinion on the results; identify, sign and date the report.
> - **Internal user report (1720.04)** requires only four things at a minimum: any new/different assumption or method (and the rationale), any reservation, any deviation from accepted actuarial practice, and the actuary's identity.
> - **The overlap:** reservations, deviations from accepted actuarial practice, identity, and non-status-quo assumptions survive in both. What the external report adds is the fuller apparatus of purpose/users, suitability for other purposes, model limitations, margins for adverse deviations, alternative-assumption effects, subsequent events, consistency with prior periodic reports, and dual opinions on assumptions/methods and on results.

### B3. Explain how an external user report handles its audience, and why describing purpose and users matters.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 1710: the actuary should describe the work, its purpose, and its users; users are the addressee(s) plus any others explicitly identified.
> - 1710.15: with more than one user, have regard to the information of value to **each**.
> - 1710.14: describing purpose and users lets others judge suitability to their needs and **may avoid unintended use** — reinforced by saying that use of the report may not be suitable for another purpose.
> - 1710.13: detail depends mainly on user needs; a separate report may suit a particular user (usually a regulator) wanting far more detail than others. 1710.12: description of the work usually includes the calculation date and the numerical result, and cites the law if the work is mandated.
> - 1710.06: greater formality/detail where interests of the external user and the client/employer differ.
> - **Internal user contrast (1720.02–.03):** abbreviation is driven by the internal user's familiarity with the subject, with less abbreviation expected where the internal user is the client.

### B4. What are the advantages and disadvantages of oral reporting, and how should the actuary mitigate them?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Advantage/usefulness (1730 Definition):** oral reporting, especially to an internal user, is both useful and inevitable in some situations.
> - **Disadvantage:** the actuary and the user may have differing recollections of what was reported.
> - **Mitigation:** good practice is to confirm an oral report in writing — especially when there is an external user — or to record it in documentation.
> - **Crucially, abbreviation is not a lesser standard (1730.02):** except for signature and report date, the standards are the same for oral and written reports, and the external or internal user report requirements apply equally.

### B5. Explain the distinction between a summary report and an external user report, and when a summary report is used.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - A summary report is prepared only **where required by practice-specific standards** (1740), and those standards specify the language to be used.
> - Its **purpose is to simplify the actuary's communication with users**.
> - It may be **incorporated** in a report prepared by the actuary's employer or client — e.g. the financial statements of an insurer, a pension plan, or a public personal injury compensation plan.
> - **It does not constitute an external user report** — so the full 1710 apparatus (users, suitability for other purposes, opinions, assumptions and margins, subsequent events, reservations, signature and date) is not what is being triggered.
> - **Example of the use:** SOP 2230's actuary's report *is* a summary report as described in 1740, and it is framed around policy liabilities rather than insurance contract liabilities, with an opinion on appropriateness of the liabilities and fairness of their presentation.

### B6. Explain how the 2230 report presents a reservation, and the main situations in which the standard reporting language is modified.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **"Reference" wording** points to the paragraph in the report that provides the additional explanation needed for fair presentation. **Modified wording** restates the qualification in the report itself; 2230.02 says the standard language is modified to report with reservation.
> - Situations calling for modified wording (2230.19–.29) include:
> - A new appointment using the predecessor actuary's work without taking responsibility for it — with doubts about that work after review leading to a more serious reservation.
> - The impracticality of restating (e.g. a change in the methodology for measuring coverage units used to allocate and recognize the contractual service margin).
> - A takeover of an insurer whose records did not provide sufficient and reliable data, creating an unusual degree of uncertainty.
> - Financial statements reporting policy liabilities materially different from those the actuary calculated and reported — disclose the difference in amounts and identify where the explanation is found, including important reasons where possible.
> - A material change in assumption or methodology not disclosed in the statements (e.g. the methodology for measuring the risk adjustment) — modify the opinion paragraph to disclose.
> - **Backstop (2230.13–.15):** if fair presentation requires explanation, the notes to the financial statements should provide it (including, where appropriate, the effect on the statements); in their absence the actuary provides it through a reservation in reporting. Helpful test: "Will explanation enhance the user's understanding of the insurer's financial statements?"

### B7. Compare a change in valuation methodology with a change in assumptions, from the perspective of consistency across reporting periods.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Meaningful comparability requires **consistency**, achieved by **restating** prior-period items previously reported on an inconsistent basis (2230.16–.18).
> - **Disclosure of the inconsistency is a less desirable alternative** to restating.
> - A change in **valuation methodology** may create an inconsistency.
> - A change in **assumptions** reflecting a changed expected outlook does **not** create an inconsistency — though if material, fair presentation requires disclosure.
> - A change in assumptions resulting from the application of **new standards** **may** create an inconsistency.

### B8. Explain when the actuary may take responsibility for an assumption or methodology set by another party, and when the actuary would not.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 2210.05: assumptions or methodology set or prescribed by the principal (or another party) for IFRS 17 work are treated as the actuary's **use of another person's work** under subsection 1510.
> - The actuary would **not** take responsibility if it conflicts with what would be appropriate for the purpose of the actuarial services, or the actuary is unable — or not qualified — to judge its appropriateness without performing a substantial amount of additional work beyond the scope of the assignment.
> - 2230.30 (**no reservation required**): where the actuary uses an assumption or methodology set by another party but takes responsibility for the work, the opinion paragraph is **not** modified. Examples:
> - The CIO selects different reference portfolios for groups of contracts with the same liquidity characteristics — both reasonably representative as IFRS 17 requires — even though the actuary sees an unnecessary inconsistency.
> - The risk adjustment reflects the insurer's requirements on longevity risk even though the actuary believes more compensation is warranted.
> - The CFO classifies expenses as "directly attributable" (material impact) where the actuary would not.
> - Common thread: the actuary has confidence in the person's qualifications, competence, integrity and objectivity, and the view is reasonable.
> - 1710.22: disclose any diminution of responsibility caused by an engagement whose terms require a deviation from accepted actuarial practice.

### B9. What should the appointed actuary consider before accepting and continuing an appointed actuary engagement?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 2430 (Definition): Section 1300 applies **rigoriously** to the engagement.
> - **Qualifications, experience and knowledge** go beyond technical understanding to include maturity, communication with other actuaries, discussions at Institute meetings, and familiarity with conditions internal and external to the insurer — plus communication skills.
> - An actuary accepting an engagement **for the first time** may wish to arrange professional, formal and timely access to another actuary with experience as an appointed actuary.
> - The insurer's **directors should understand and accept** the actuary's role and its requirements for time, resources and access to information; the actuary may wish **written confirmation** of that understanding and acceptance unless the role is part of the insurer's corporate culture.
> - **Information needed** and the **process to assure timely receipt** of it (see A13).
> - Part 1000 applies to any work within the scope of Section 2400 (2420.01); "senior management" and "directors" are defined in 2410.

## Part C — Scenarios and Judgment Calls

Source: [[Reporting external user report]], [[Reporting]], [[Report on matters requiring rectification]], [[Communication with the auditor]], [[Report to the directors]], [[Reporting internal user report]]

### C1. The AA discovers, six weeks after issuing a funding valuation report, an arithmetic error in the projections that would materially change the recommendations. What does SOP 1710 require?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 1710.04: withdraw or amend the report if information coming to hand after the report date invalidates it.
> - 1710.41: additional information may be **external** or an **internal discovery of an error**; classify it like subsequent events — it affects the report if the event would have to be reflected in data, assumptions or methods, but not if it only changes the entity's outlook leading to different assumptions at the next calculation date.
> - 1710.43: if a funding report's recommendations would change materially due to a discovered error, **withdrawing or amending may be appropriate**.
> - Contrast (1710.43): no action on a prior report once superseded; if the plan's financial position is materially affected but its funding is not, disclosure to users may suffice; if an assumption later proves obviously erroneous but was reasonable at the report date, typically reflect it in a subsequent report.
> - 1710.42: determine whether the event invalidates the report; if not, consider informing some or all users; if it does, withdraw or amend — seeking the client/employer's agreement on notifying users and on an amended or replacement report, otherwise consider legal advice (all users should so be informed to the extent practical and useful).
> - 1710.40: there is no obligation to seek information after the report date, but information that comes to hand must be considered — here it reveals a calculation error.

### C2. A valuation reveals that the insurer's financial statements present policy liabilities materially different from those the AA calculated and reported. What must the AA's report say?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 2230.19–.29: use **modified wording** to disclose the difference in amounts and identify where the explanation is found, including important reasons where possible.
> - 2230.13–.15: if fair presentation requires explanation, the notes to the financial statements should provide it (including, where appropriate, the effect on the statements); in their absence, the actuary provides it through a **reservation in reporting**.
> - Helpful test: "Will explanation enhance the user's understanding of the insurer's financial statements?"
> - Balance the disclosure against 1710.07 (too much disclosure exaggerates minor matters or makes the report hard to read).

### C3. The auditor asks the AA to communicate with them at various stages of the year — drafting common features of the two reports, a report with reservation, presentation of the policy liabilities and other items valued, and treatment of subsequent events. Is this required, and what else follows?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 2230.11–.12: communication with the auditor is **desirable at various stages**, covering mutual use of each other's work; drafting of common features; drafting a report with reservation; presentation of insurance contract and other policy liabilities and other items valued by the actuary; and treatment of subsequent events.
> - **Uniformity of common features avoids confusion** — addressees (policyholders of a mutual insurer; participating policyholders and shareholders of a stock insurer), years referenced (usually only the current year), and report date (the same date entails taking account of the same subsequent events).
> - Note the strength of words: 2460 is **"would be desirable"** — encouraged, not mandatory — but continual communication with the auditor per the CIA/CICA Joint Policy Statement is part of the process to identify and assure timely receipt of information under 2430.06.

### C4. The AA identifies an adverse condition that does **not** threaten the insurer's financial condition. Is a report to senior management required?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **No.** 2440: there would be **no report to senior management** for an adverse condition that does not threaten the insurer's financial condition.
> - **Informal notification and consultation would usually precede, and may obviate, that report.**
> - Remember also that sensitivity of financial condition to adverse conditions and events varies among insurers, and so does the magnitude of the conditions and events that may threaten it — so the threshold judgment is insurer-specific.

### C5. The AA sends a report to senior management on a matter requiring rectification, with a recommended deadline. Senior management takes no suitable action before the deadline expires. What must the AA do, and with whom should the AA have communicated?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 2440: if there is **no suitable rectification** by that deadline or its extension, the AA should **report the matter to the insurer's regulator**.
> - The report to the regulator describes the AA's **investigation**, the **report to senior management**, and **senior management's response** to that report; the AA would **advise the directors** of the report to the regulator.
> - In the case of a **Canadian insurer**, a **copy** of the report to senior management goes to the **directors**; depending on jurisdiction, the law may also require the report be provided to the regulator.
> - 2460: communication with the auditor is **desirable** when the AA makes such a report to senior management.
> - 2440: the report to senior management should describe the threatening condition or event and the assumptions and methods in the investigation, and it is desirable that it include recommendations for rectification; the deadline should allow time that is reasonable in the circumstances.

### C6. The AA is asked to give an unqualified opinion that the dividend scale is fair to participating policy owners, but on review the scale does not conform to the dividend policy. What does SOP 2450 require?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - 2450: the dividend scale report considers the **conformity of the scale to the policy** and its **fairness** to participating policy owners; the dividend policy report considers the fairness of the policy to participating policy owners.
> - Where the law requires a fairness opinion, the **unqualified** opinion is that the policy/method was reviewed in accordance with accepted actuarial practice in Canada and pursuant to the guidance of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions, and is, in the actuary's opinion, fair (and, for allocations, fair and equitable) to the participating policyholders.
> - If the AA is **unable to issue an unqualified opinion**, the wording of the opinion is **adjusted to reflect the necessary qualification** (see 2450.07 for model wording).
> - The AA reports at least yearly to the directors or, if they so delegate, their audit committee.

## Part D — Exam-Style Questions

Source: [[Reporting external user report]], [[Reporting internal user report]], [[Reporting oral report]], [[Summary report]], [[General]], [[Reporting]], [[Accepting and continuing an engagement]], [[Communication with the auditor]], [[Report on matters requiring rectification]], [[Report to the directors]]

### D1. Describe what an external user report must contain under SOP 1710, and explain how the actuary should decide how much to disclose.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Required content (1710 Definition):**
> - Identify the client or employer.
> - Describe the work, its purpose and its users.
> - Say that use of the report may not be suitable for another purpose.
> - Say whether the work is in accordance with accepted actuarial practice in Canada and, if not, disclose the deviation.
> - If useful, disclose any unusual application of accepted actuarial practice.
> - If supported by a model, disclose model limitations relevant to the intended purpose.
> - Disclose any aspect of the work for which the actuary does not take responsibility.
> - Describe each material assumption, including the extent of any margin for adverse deviations, and provide the rationale for each.
> - For non-model/non-data assumptions differing from the status quo, disclose them and, if practical, useful and appropriate, the effect of alternatives.
> - Describe the methods; in a periodic report disclose inconsistencies with prior reports and the rationale.
> - Describe subsequent events not taken into account; disclose any reservation.
> - Express an opinion on the assumptions and methods and on the results.
> - Identify and sign the report, and date it.
> - **Balancing disclosure (.02–.11):**
> - Description or disclosure may sit in material accompanying the report or plausibly available to users.
> - The actuary should respond to a user's request for explanation, except if contrary to the terms of engagement.
> - Balance is a two-way risk: too little deprives the user; too much exaggerates minor matters, implies diminished responsibility, or makes the report hard to read.
> - The criterion is what best serves the user's understanding and decision-making — not merely what the user wants.
> - Consider sensitivity of results to variations in key assumptions where practical, useful and consistent with the engagement.
> - Disclosure need not be in the report itself unless its importance warrants it.
> - Avoid unintended reservations: approximation and use of another's work are usual; immaterial deviations need not be disclosed.
> - A duty of confidentiality in an appropriate engagement supersedes any conflicting requirement.
> - **Practical points:** withdraw or amend if later information invalidates the report; be relatively formal and detailed where the actuary does not communicate directly with users or where external-user and client/employer interests differ; begin opinions with "In my opinion"; a signed "Statement of Opinion" section is convenient; consider all information available up to the report date when the report date is after the calculation date.

### D2. Compare and contrast the four report types recognized in Section 1700 — external user report, internal user report, oral report and summary report.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **External user report (1710):** the fullest standard — full identification of client, work, purpose and users; suitability caveat; accepted actuarial practice statement and any deviation; material assumptions with margins for adverse deviations and rationale; methods; opinions; reservations; subsequent events; signature and date. Range of appropriate reports is **relatively narrow**; be relatively formal and detailed where the actuary does not communicate directly with users or external-user and client/employer interests differ.
> - **Internal user report (1720):** may appropriately **abbreviate** the recommendations for external user reports; the range of appropriate reports is **wider**, running from near-identical to external reports down to an informal, abbreviated, even oral report for a well-versed representative of the actuary's employer or client who communicates frequently with the actuary. Abbreviation is efficient provided complete and clear communication is not compromised. Consider the user's familiarity and the circumstances; less abbreviation is expected where the internal user is the client. Minimum disclosure: new/different assumption or method plus rationale, any reservation, any deviation from accepted actuarial practice, and identity.
> - **Oral report (1730):** useful and inevitable in some situations, especially for internal users; the standards are the **same** as for written reports except for signature and report date, so external or internal requirements apply equally. Disadvantage: differing recollections. Remedy: confirm in writing (especially with an external user) or record in documentation.
> - **Summary report (1740):** prepared only where required by practice-specific standards, which also specify the language used; purpose is to **simplify** communication with users; may be incorporated in a report prepared by the actuary's employer or client (e.g. financial statements of an insurer, pension plan, or public personal injury compensation plan); **does not constitute an external user report**. SOP 2230's actuary's report is such a summary report.
> - **Contrast in one line:** the four differ chiefly in **formality, permitted abbreviation, and audience** — not in the underlying obligation that what is communicated be complete, clear and not misleading.

### D3. Describe the appointed actuary's reporting obligations: to the directors, on matters requiring rectification, and to the regulator. Include when communication with the auditor is desirable.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **To the directors (2450):** at least yearly, to the directors or, if they so delegate, their **audit committee** — on the insurer's financial position and financial condition. If required by law, also: the method of allocation of income and expenses to each participating account, and the management of the participating account(s), dividend policy and dividend scales; and, where adjustable policies are in force, the directors' criteria established or amended for changes to premium or charge, amount of insurance, or surrender value. Each report tests fairness/equity: allocations must be fair **and equitable**; dividend scale conformity to policy and fairness; adjustable-policy adjustments conformity to the criteria and fairness. Where a fairness opinion is required, the unqualified opinion references accepted actuarial practice in Canada and the guidance of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions; otherwise the wording is adjusted to reflect the qualification. For a **foreign insurer**, report at least yearly to its **chief agent for Canada** on financial position and financial condition.
> - **On matters requiring rectification (2440):** identify and monitor matters that may threaten financial condition; investigate and, as required by law, report to **senior management**, with a **copy to the directors** for a Canadian insurer and, depending on jurisdiction, to the **regulator**. Include recommendations for rectification and a **deadline** that allows time reasonable in the circumstances, extendable if appropriate. Frequency and intensity of monitoring depend on the threatening conditions and the circumstances of the insurer — a quarterly review would usually be a minimum. No report is required for an adverse condition that does not threaten financial condition; informal notification and consultation usually precede and may obviate it.
> - **To the regulator (2440):** if there is no suitable rectification by the deadline or its extension, report to the regulator, describing the investigation, the report to senior management, and senior management's response; advise the directors of that report.
> - **Auditor (2460):** communication is **desirable** — not mandatory — when the AA reports to senior management on a matter requiring rectification, or makes an **unfavourable** report on financial condition. Continual communication with the auditor per the CIA/CICA Joint Policy Statement is part of the process to assure timely receipt of information under 2430.06. "Senior management" (2410) means the CEO, CFO and chief risk officer for a Canadian insurer; the chief agent for Canada and the person designated as responsible for the Canadian operation for a foreign insurer.

### D4. Describe the appointed actuary's report under SOP 2230: its nature, structure, the presentation of policy liabilities, and the treatment of unusual situations and reservations.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Nature:** it is the actuary's report — a **summary report** under 1740. It should conform to relevant Canadian federal and provincial legislation requiring the actuary to value **policy liabilities**, not only insurance contract liabilities; describe the valuation and presentation of policy liabilities for IFRS financial statements; include the actuary's opinion on the **appropriateness** of those liabilities and the **fairness** of their presentation; and describe the actuary's role in preparing the financial statements if not described there or in the MD&A. Effective January 1, 2023.
> - **Structure (2230.02):** without reservation — a **scope paragraph** plus an **opinion paragraph**; otherwise the standard language is **modified** to report with reservation. Square-bracketed wording is variable and may be adjusted for interim statements and terminology.
> - **Presentation (2230.03–.04):** IFRS valuation requires valuing items beyond total policy liabilities, and all items derived from that valuation reported in the statement of financial position, statement of financial performance, statement of changes in equity, statement of cash flows and the notes are part of the presentation — e.g. the liability for remaining coverage presented as insurance revenue; coverage units used to allocate release of the contractual service margin; the components (PV of future cash flows, risk adjustment for non-financial risk, contractual service margin) for each of the four portfolios; and reconciliation of the change in the contractual service margin or loss component.
> - **Roles (2230.05–.08):** the **insurer** is responsible for identification, combination, aggregation, separation, recognition and derecognition of contracts, the choice of measurement approach and assumptions, the measurement calculations and the disclosures. Where legislation requires, the **actuary** is responsible for valuing policy liabilities and reporting to policyholders and shareholders, so the report should describe that role if the statements or MD&A omit it. MD&A may also describe formal responsibilities beyond statement preparation, e.g. annual financial condition testing and reporting to the directors.
> - **Unusual situations (2230.13–.15):** the report signals whether presentation is fair. If fair presentation requires explanation, the notes should provide it (including, where appropriate, the effect on the statements); otherwise the actuary provides it by reservation. Test: "Will explanation enhance the user's understanding of the insurer's financial statements?" Examples include capital appropriated or repatriated on the actuary's advice; off-balance-sheet obligations (e.g. contingent policy liabilities on market conduct); restatement of prior-period items; inconsistency among periods; impracticality of restating; an unusual relationship between current and expected future items; a methodology change with no current effect but expected future effect; a difference between the insurer's practices (e.g. dividend scales) and those assumed; and a subsequent event.
> - **Consistency (2230.16–.18):** achieve comparability by **restating** prior-period items reported on an inconsistent basis; disclosure of the inconsistency is a less desirable alternative. A methodology change may create inconsistency; an assumption change reflecting a changed outlook does not (but if material, fair presentation requires disclosure); an assumption change from applying new standards may create inconsistency.
> - **Reservations (2230.19–.30):** "reference" wording points to the paragraph giving the needed explanation; modified wording covers a new appointment relying on the predecessor's work without responsibility (with doubts producing a more serious reservation); impracticality of restating (e.g. coverage-unit methodology); a takeover where records did not provide sufficient and reliable data, creating unusual uncertainty; statements showing policy liabilities materially different from those the actuary calculated (disclose amounts and where the explanation is found, with important reasons where possible); and a material assumption/methodology change not disclosed in the statements (e.g. risk adjustment methodology). By contrast, **no reservation** is required where the actuary uses an assumption or methodology set by another party but takes responsibility for it — e.g. the CIO's choice of different but both reasonably representative reference portfolios, a longevity-driven risk adjustment the actuary thinks under-compensated, or the CFO's "directly attributable" expense classification — provided the actuary is confident in the person's qualifications, competence, integrity and objectivity and the view is reasonable.
> - **Alignment with the auditor (2230.11–.12):** uniformity of addressees, years referenced (usually only the current year) and report date (so the same subsequent events are taken into account) avoids confusion; communication with the auditor is desirable at the stages listed in C3.
> - **Cross-reference (2210.04):** the IFRS 17 risk adjustment for non-financial risk is **not** a provision for adverse deviations.

### D5. Describe the requirements on an actuary who is accepting and continuing an appointed actuary engagement, and explain why the standard treats these as rigorous.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Rigour (2430 Definition):** Section 1300 applies rigorously to the engagement, and Part 1000 applies to any work within the scope of Section 2400 (2420.01). "Senior management" and "directors" are defined in 2410.
> - **Qualifications, experience and knowledge** go beyond technical understanding to include the awareness that comes with maturity, communication with other actuaries, discussions at Institute meetings, familiarity with conditions internal and external to the insurer, and communication skills.
> - **First-time appointees** may wish to arrange professional, formal and timely access to another actuary with experience as an appointed actuary.
> - **Directors' understanding and acceptance** of the actuary's role and its requirements for time, resources and access to information; written confirmation is advisable unless the role is part of the insurer's corporate culture.
> - **Information needed:** records, accounts, documents and oral briefings giving an understanding of operations, obligations and available resources — including in-force policy and outstanding claim files with reinsurance; policy provisions and communications with policy owners; past experience and past financial data; communications with auditors and regulators; pricing, underwriting and accounting practice; claims settlement practice including case estimate practice, and cost; asset-liability and capital management practice; enterprise risk management policy; and the ORSA report.
> - **Process to assure timely receipt:** understanding the insurer's decision-making; continual communication with management who can supply information; and continual communication with the auditor in accordance with the CIA/CICA Joint Policy Statement (which is also the basis of the "desirable" auditor communication under 2460).
> - **Link to monitoring (2440):** because the AA must identify and monitor matters threatening financial condition, with a quarterly review usually a minimum, and must be able to investigate and report, the adequacy and timeliness of information are central to the role.
