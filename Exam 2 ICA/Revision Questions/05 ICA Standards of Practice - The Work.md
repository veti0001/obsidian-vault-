# ICA Standards of Practice — The Work (Materiality, 1400, 1500, 1600) — Revision Questions

## Part A — Definitions and Recall
Source: [[Materiality]], [[Approximation]], [[Control]], [[Data]], [[Documentation]], [[Event]], [[Model]], [[Quality assurance]], [[Reasonableness of results]], [[Subsequent events]], [[Actuary's use of another person's work]], [[Auditor's use of an actuary's work]], [[Review or repeat of another actuary's work]], [[Assumptions]], [[Comparison of current and prior assumptions]], [[Methods]], [[Provision for adverse deviations]]

### A1. What is the definition of materiality under the CIA Standards?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Deviation from a recommendation or explanatory text is accepted actuarial practice if the effect of so doing is not material.
> - "Material" has its ordinary meaning, judged from the point of view of a user, having regard for the purpose of the work.

### A2. When does a simpler practice requiring less time and expense remain acceptable despite producing different numbers?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - When the result does not differ materially from applying the recommendation.

### A3. What is the key principle for netting when assessing materiality between separately reported items?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - It is not appropriate to net items that are reported separately.
> - It is appropriate to net components within a separately reported item.

### A4. Define approximation.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - An approximation is appropriate if it reduces the cost of, reduces the time needed for, or improves the actuary's control over, work without affecting the result.

### A5. What is the requirement for control procedures in calculations?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Control procedures that detect errors and decrease the effect of errors should be performed for calculations.

### A6. Define the actuary's responsibility with respect to data sufficiency and reliability.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The actuary should apply such procedures as are necessary to arrive at a conclusion as to the sufficiency and reliability of the data.

### A7. What must the actuary use best efforts to compile and secure?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The actuary should use best efforts to compile and secure the retention of appropriate documentation.

### A8. When should events definitive or virtually definitive on or before the calculation date be reflected?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - They should be reflected in the work, unless the purpose of the work is to reflect the situation prior to the event.

### A9. State the two requirements when using a model.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Choose a model appropriate to the purpose and requirements of the work.
> - Understand any limitations that might make results inappropriate or misleading.

### A10. What is required for quality assurance prior to releasing work?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The actuary should implement appropriate quality assurance processes prior to the release of work to users.

### A11. What must the actuary do regarding reasonableness of results?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The actuary should examine the reasonableness of a calculation's result.

### A12. What should the actuary do if a subsequent event reveals a data defect or calculation error?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The actuary should correct any data defect or calculation error that is revealed by a subsequent event.

### A13. Define "use and take responsibility" for another person's work (1510).

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The actuary should use and take responsibility for another person's work if such actions are justified.

### A14. What standards apply to a review engagement at the instigation of a user (1530)?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The standards apply to a review engagement at the instigation of a user (not to internal firm quality control alone).
> - If terms permit, the first actuary should cooperate with the reviewer.
> - They also apply mutatis mutandis to a repeat engagement.

### A15. What is the actuary's obligation regarding selecting assumptions (1620)?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The actuary should identify and select each assumption needed for the work, except those prescribed, mandated by law, or stipulated by the terms of engagement.

### A16. When must assumptions be consistent between periodic calculations (1640)?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Unless the actuary reports the inconsistency, assumptions for a periodic report should be consistent with those of the prior calculation.

### A17. What must the actuary do when selecting a method (1610)?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - The actuary should select a method that takes account of the circumstances affecting the work.

### A18. Under what circumstances may provisions for adverse deviations be included (1630)?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Only to the extent required by the terms of engagement, mandated by law, or prescribed by practice-specific standards.

### A19. Define "inquiring professional" and "responding professional" in the context of auditor/actuary use (1520).

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Inquiring professional: the actuary or auditor using the work of the other.
> - Responding professional: the one whose work is being used.

## Part B — Explain, Compare, Advantages and Disadvantages
Source: [[Materiality]], [[Approximation]], [[Control]], [[Data]], [[Model]], [[Quality assurance]], [[Subsequent events]], [[Actuary's use of another person's work]], [[Review or repeat of another actuary's work]], [[Assumptions]], [[Methods]]

### B1. Explain the tension between "equity" and "practicality" when setting materiality (e.g. winding up a pension plan or policy dividend scale).

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Materiality may vary among users and uses, with conflicts (e.g. affecting each participant's share vs. practicality).
> - The standard depends on the user's reasonable expectations consistent with the purpose of work.

### B2. Compare approximation and assumption in terms of effect on the result and what happens if the actuary cannot assess the error.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Approximation is appropriate if it doesn't affect the result.
> - If the actuary cannot assess resulting error, the approximation becomes, in effect, an assumption.

### B3. Explain why control procedures are especially important for certain types of calculations.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Data-intensive, complex, involve physically separate steps, or combinations are prone to error.
> - Controls prevent/detect errors and help ensure consistency with other related work.

### B4. Explain the considerations in choosing between data specific to circumstances and other relevant data, and how to balance homogeneity vs size.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Use specific data when available/relevant; else use industry/population data with suitable adjustments.
> - Balance homogeneity (better representation) against data size (credibility).
> - Blend if credibility of specific data is lower than needed.

### B5. Outline strategies to mitigate model risk, including when dealing with third-party models.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Validate implementation uses data/assumptions as intended; verify methods function as intended; test reasonableness against alternative models; compare components.
> - Understand model purpose, basic operations, sensitivities, limitations, strengths, training (if relevant), weaknesses.
> - Range of strategies may be more limited for third-party models or limited access to intermediate results.

### B6. Contrast "use and take responsibility" vs "use but not take responsibility" for another person's work.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Take responsibility: justified actions; not required to report use (but may disclose key considerations); other person remains responsible too.
> - Not take responsibility: report reliance and rationale; do not claim expertise of other person; must examine for evident shortcomings and reasonableness/consistency; otherwise avoid use.

### B7. Explain the key differences between a pre-release review and a post-release review (1530), and when discussion might be limited.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Pre-release: opportunity to suggest improvement before release.
> - Post-release: only future improvement possible; may require withdrawal/revision.
> - Discussion may be limited/precluded in evidence work, litigation/mediation, judicial/regulatory investigation, or when client wants independent reports.

### B8. Differentiate "model assumptions", "data assumptions/transformations/adjustments", and "assumptions other than model and data assumptions" with examples.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Model assumptions: quantitative assumptions in model (contingent events, investment returns/economic matters, numerical parameters).
> - Data assumptions/transformations/adjustments: needed to relieve insufficiency/unreliability (e.g. spouse age relationship from sampling).
> - Other assumptions: legal/economic/demographic/social environment (qualitative); status quo continuation usually appropriate unless none or expected to change.

### B9. Explain "independently reasonable and appropriate in the aggregate" and what to do if an assumption is prescribed/mandated.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Each assumption independently reasonable; avoid inconsistent or same-direction bias making aggregate unreasonable.
> - If prescribed/mandated by law or stipulated, do not compensate by modifying others; remaining assumptions reasonable in aggregate and independently reasonable to extent possible.

### B10. Compare the definition of "consistency" of assumptions for pension funding advice vs insurance contract liabilities (financial reporting).

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Pension funding (advice): consistent if numerically the same as prior calculation date.
> - Insurance contract liabilities (financial reporting): consistent if (a) best estimate reflects conditions/outlook at respective dates; (b) margins reflect risks at respective dates; (c) located at same point in range of accepted actuarial practice.

## Part C — Scenarios and Judgment Calls
Source: [[Materiality]], [[Approximation]], [[Control]], [[Data]], [[Documentation]], [[Event]], [[Model]], [[Quality assurance]], [[Reasonableness of results]], [[Subsequent events]], [[Actuary's use of another person's work]], [[Assumptions]], [[Methods]]

### C1. Scenario: The appointed actuary discovers that using a simpler approach overstates premium liabilities and materially understates claim liabilities, but the total insurance contract liabilities are not materially affected. How should materiality be assessed for reporting?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Do not net items that are reported separately.
> - If both premium liabilities and claim liabilities are reported separately, the overstatement and understatement are each material and cannot be offset.

### C2. Scenario: Missing plan member birth dates make precise calculation impractical. The actuary samples similar data to impute values. Is this acceptable? Under what condition?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Sampling/extrapolation from similar situations may be an appropriate approximation if it reduces effort without affecting the result.
> - If appropriateness is doubtful or error cannot be assessed, it becomes effectively an assumption and must be handled accordingly.

### C3. Scenario: A complex valuation has multiple parallel processing steps and manual entries. What controls should the actuary consider?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Ensure all steps coordinated/performed/checked; prevent data corruption; avoid inadvertent changes to established procedures; track changes orderly; validate model runs.
> - Use tools like random sampling, testing on independent datasets, spot checks, audit trails; perform model validation.

### C4. Scenario: The actuary becomes aware after the report date of an event that invalidates the report. What action is required?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - If the event invalidates the report, the actuary should withdraw or amend the report and consider informing the user.

### C5. Scenario: A subsequent event occurred between calculation date and report date. It was precipitated by a sudden catastrophe that causes reinsurer insolvency. How should the actuary classify/treat it depending on purpose?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - If precipitated by catastrophe (not culmination of gradual pre-calculation deterioration), it makes the entity different after calculation date.
> - If the purpose is to report as at calculation date, do not take it into account but report it.
> - If the purpose is to report as it will be as a result of the event, take it into account and describe it with appropriate cut-off.

### C6. Scenario: The user wants to rely on a third-party's model but gives limited access to intermediate results and training details. What considerations apply?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Range of strategies to mitigate model risk may be more limited.
> - Document how the actuary assessed the model as appropriate for purpose; understand key operations/sensitivities/limitations/weaknesses as much as practical.
> - Validate at outcome level where possible; consider model risk/significance in diligence.

### C7. Scenario: An actuary uses another actuary's work but cannot communicate directly with the original performer. What should be done?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Assess considerations via another person with sufficient expertise and communication with original performer.
> - Consider documentation availability, QA/control processes (if available), qualifications/competence/objectivity, purpose/limitations, credibility of report/reservations.
> - Decide whether to take responsibility (justify) or use without taking responsibility (report reliance, examine for shortcomings).

### C8. Scenario: A salary freeze is announced shortly after calculation date. The actuary must decide whether it's "information about entity as it was" or "makes entity different after". What factors matter?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - If it corrects excessive salaries, may provide information about entity as it was at calculation date.
> - If it deals with recent problem indicating change in conditions, it makes entity different after calculation date.
> - Consider whether effect is lasting or will be compensated by higher salaries later.

### C9. Scenario: For a periodic valuation, economic conditions have shifted materially since prior valuation but the actuary is tempted to keep some assumptions numerically identical for stability. What standard applies?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Assumptions must be consistent unless inconsistency is reported (1640).
> - For insurance contract liabilities (financial reporting), consistency means reflecting conditions/outlook and risks at respective dates and same point in range; not necessarily numerically identical.
> - Quantify effect of inconsistency if practical/useful/appropriate.

### C10. Scenario: The terms of engagement require a provision for adverse deviations, but the actuary believes it's not needed. What action is required?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Include PFAD only to extent required by terms of engagement/mandated by law/prescribed by practice-specific standards (1630).
> - If required by terms, include as specified even if actuary's view differs; otherwise do not include.

## Part D — Exam-Style Questions
Source: [[Materiality]], [[Data]], [[Model]], [[Subsequent events]], [[Actuary's use of another person's work]], [[Quality assurance]], [[Assumptions]], [[Methods]], [[Control]], [[Documentation]]

### D1. "Materiality is an all-or-nothing test applied globally." Critique this statement with reference to the standards.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - False. Materiality is judged from user perspective relative to purpose; can vary by user, use, entity size, threshold proximity, work type.
> - Cannot net separately reported items; netting only allowed within separately reported items.
> - Different standards apply (calculation vs reporting) and level must relate to intended uses.

### D2. Outline the actuary's obligations when sufficient and reliable data cannot be obtained. Include rectification steps and reporting implications.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Attempt to rectify: obtain corrected/more complete/alternative/additional/supplementary data; make assumptions for incomplete data; make adjustments/transformations.
> - Consider reporting with reservation re data, or declining to perform work if still insufficient/unreliable.
> - If assumptions/adjustments may cause material uncertainty or statistical bias, report so and report limitations where appropriate.
> - Not normally required to audit or discover falsified/misleading data; consider provider qualifications.

### D3. You are reviewing another actuary's work as part of a user-instigated review. Describe your responsibilities regarding discussion with the first actuary, differences, and reporting.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Discuss with first actuary as soon as practical if terms permit (unless superfluous); attempt to resolve differences.
> - Report if work is within range despite disagreement; if limitation constrained quality; if discussion improved work; if work not within range.
> - Report material differences with explanation; non-material differences usually not reported unless access to different data/info/resources/time constraints reduced uncertainty.
> - Qualified reviewer (prima facie qualified if could do original work); independence enhances objectivity.

### D4. Explain how a model is determined to be "appropriate and used appropriately" under the standards. Include documentation considerations for complex models.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Appropriate if enables understanding complex reality at reasonable cost while preserving aspects important to work; specification fits purpose; implementation verified as accurate representation; inputs consistent; interpretation per specification.
> - Complex models require extra diligence (financial significance/complexity/uncertainty); document intended purpose, appropriateness of specification, limitations, testing of implementation, mitigating strategies for model risk.
> - Document sufficiently for another knowledgeable actuary to assess judgments and reasonableness of run/key results.
> - Consider risk classification, validation against observed data over time, assumptions/training data/hyperparameters/transformations.

### D5. A definitive decision is made after calculation date but effective on/before calculation date. How should this be treated under subsequent events rules? What if the event makes the entity different after calculation date but the purpose is to report as at calculation date?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - If retroactively makes entity different at calculation date (definitive decision effective on/before), effect same as if known on/before - do not report as subsequent event; reflect in work.
> - If makes entity different after calculation date and purpose is to report as at calculation date: do not take into account, but report the subsequent event (affects future operations/subsequent calculations).
> - May report alternative calculation if useful; establish cut-off if taking into account for forward-looking purpose.
