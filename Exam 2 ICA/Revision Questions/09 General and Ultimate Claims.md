# General and Ultimate Claims — Revision Questions

## Part A — Definitions and Recall
Source: [[Definition]], [[Materiality]], [[Subsequent events]], [[P&C]]

### A1. Ultimate value

Define an ultimate value, state why we need ultimate values, list what an ultimate can include, and state the required specifications.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Definition:** the total amount that will be incurred for a certain amount.
> - **Purpose:** we need ultimate values since we do not know the total cost of the insurance yet.
> - **Can include:** claims; claim-related expenses; counts (ultimate counts = closed counts + open counts + IBNR counts); average value of claims; recoveries; ratios.
> - **Required specifications:** time frame (AY, CY, RY, PY) and date used (as of ...).

### A2. Ultimate claims — composition and formulas

List the four components of ultimate claims and give the equivalent ways of writing ultimate claims.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Composition:** cumulative claim payments; case estimate for claims that have been reported; development on case estimates; IBNYR.
> - Ultimate Claims = Cumulative claim payment + Case estimates + IBNER + IBNYR
> - Ultimate Claims = Reported claims + IBNR
> - IBNR = IBNER + IBNYR
> - Claim liability = Case estimates + IBNR

### A3. Case estimate

What is a case estimate, who sets it, how does it behave over time, and how does a reinsurer treat it?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Also known as **case outstanding** or **case reserved**.
> - Estimates of the amounts yet to be paid on reported claims.
> - Set by claim adjusters.
> - Will change over time as more information is gathered.
> - For reinsurers, a **buffer** (additional case reserve) is added on top of what the insurer has put as the case reserve.

### A4. Reported claims and IBNER

How are reported claims defined, what is the development on reported claims called, and what else does IBNER cover?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Reported claims = Cumulative claim payment + Case estimate.
> - Development on reported claims is called **Incurred But Not Enough Reported (IBNER)**.
> - IBNER also includes provisions for claims that were **closed but may reopen** in the future.

### A5. IBNYR

What is IBNYR, and how does it relate to IBNR, ultimate claims and claim liability?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - IBNYR is **pure IBNR**.
> - IBNR = IBNER + IBNYR, so pure IBNYR is only part of total IBNR.
> - Ultimate Claims = Reported claims + IBNR (cumulative payments + case + IBNER + IBNYR).
> - Claim liability = Case estimates + IBNR, i.e. the unpaid portion only.

### A6. Materiality

Define materiality and identify the key driver in determining it.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - An omission or misstatement is **material** if the judgment of a reasonable person would have been changed or influenced by the inclusion or correction of the item, given surrounding circumstances.
> - The **user is key** to the determination of materiality.
> - Only need to consider the **intended audience** when determining the materiality threshold.

### A7. Subsequent events

Define a subsequent event, distinguish adjusting from non-adjusting events, and state the threshold for acting on it.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Definition:** an event of which an actuary first becomes aware after a **calculation date** but before the corresponding **report date**.
> - **Adjusting events:** provide evidence of conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period; need to be **included in calculations**.
> - **Non-adjusting events:** indicative of conditions that arose after the reporting period; need to be **disclosed**.
> - The event needs to be **material** to be considered.

### A8. P&C exhibits and their IFRS 17 linkages

Describe the P&C-1 / P&C-2 / P&C-3 hierarchy and state which exhibit feeds LIC and LRC.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - P&C exhibits form a **hierarchical reporting structure** for IFRS 17.
> - **P&C-1:** top-level aggregation (Statement of Financial Position, P&L, Capital & Solvency).
> - **P&C-2:** claims development triangles → feeds **LIC** (Liability for Incurred Claims) in P&C-1.
> - **P&C-3:** premium/exposure detail → feeds **LRC** (Liability for Remaining Coverage) in P&C-1.
> - **LRC** ← Premium + exposure from P&C-3.
> - **LIC** ← Best estimate + RA + discounting from P&C-2.
> - **Insurance Revenue** ← Coverage units (P&C-3) + claims emergence (P&C-2).
> - **Reinsurance Held** ← Ceded balances from both P&C-2 (claims) and P&C-3 (premium).

## Part B — Explain, Compare, Advantages and Disadvantages
Source: [[Definition]], [[Use of ultimate claims]], [[Materiality]], [[Subsequent events]], [[P&C]]

### B1. Incurred claims vs ultimate claims

Explain why incurred claims data alone is not sufficient and what the ultimate claims measure adds.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Reported claims = cumulative claim payment + case estimate, so incurred data at a point in time is only an interim measure.
> - Case estimates **will change over time** as more information is gathered.
> - Ultimate claims add the not-yet-emerged component: Ultimate Claims = Reported claims + IBNR, where IBNR = IBNER + IBNYR.
> - IBNER also carries provisions for claims that were closed but may reopen in the future.
> - We need ultimate values since we do not know the total cost of the insurance yet.

### B2. IBNER vs IBNYR

Compare IBNER and IBNYR.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **IBNER** is development on **reported** claims (cumulative claim payment + case estimate).
> - IBNER also includes provisions for claims that were **closed but may reopen** in the future.
> - **IBNYR** is **pure IBNR** — the not-yet-reported portion.
> - The two combine: IBNR = IBNER + IBNYR, and Ultimate Claims = Reported claims + IBNR.
> - Development on case estimates is the IBNER element; IBNYR is the remaining component of the ultimate not yet reported.

### B3. Uses of ultimate claims

Explain the main areas in which ultimate claim values are used and why each is needed.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Financial reporting:** liability values need to be estimated in financial reports to be in line with IFRS 17; Appointed Actuaries (AA) need to disclose their opinion on the adequacy of the reserve amount; incurred claims are needed to derive underwriting (UW) income; helps prepare financial statement disclosures.
> - **Pricing:** ultimate claims for past periods are needed to set rate levels.
> - **Financial condition analysis:** tests the ability of an insurer to meet its obligations if an extreme event happens (Exam 3 content).
> - **Planning:** used for planning and budgeting; needed to know how to allocate resources (financial and non-financial); also needed to determine employee bonuses.
> - **Merger and acquisition analyses:** needed to know the actual value of the company.

### B4. Materiality — considerations and disclosure

What should be considered when setting a materiality threshold, and what caution applies to disclosing it?

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Depends on the **needs, skill, sophistication, and experience of the intended audience** for the actuary's work.
> - **Task-specific** and not product specific.
> - Materiality threshold **should not change in time**.
> - It **varies** as a function of: size of the entity; entity's access to capital; stage of organizational life cycle; type of business (multi-line vs single line, personal lines vs commercial lines); net retention.
> - **Disclosure caution:** materiality should not be disclosed if it would create misunderstanding and confusion from the intended user.
> - The purpose of the work should be adequately defined in the work so that it cannot be misused (mainly due to the materiality level).

### B5. Adjusting vs non-adjusting subsequent events

Compare the two types of subsequent events and explain how each is handled.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Both are events first noticed after a calculation date but before the corresponding report date.
> - **Adjusting events** provide evidence of conditions that **existed at the end of the reporting period**; they are included in calculations.
> - **Non-adjusting events** are indicative of conditions that **arose after the reporting period**; they are disclosed rather than included.
> - An event is only considered if it is **material** to the calculation.
> - Correspondingly, the event must provide information about the entity as it was at the calculation date, retroactively make the entity different at the calculation date, or the purpose of the work must be to report on the entity as it will be as a result of the event.

### B6. P&C-2 vs P&C-3

Compare the roles and contents of P&C-2 and P&C-3 and explain how each connects to P&C-1.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **P&C-2** is the detailed view of claims emergence and unpaid claims supporting LIC: development triangles (paid, incurred, counts, ALAE / ULAE); unpaid claims analysis (best estimate, discounting, risk adjustment, reinsurance recoveries); large losses & catastrophe claims; layered reinsurance impacts.
> - **P&C-3** is the detailed view of written/earned premium and exposure supporting LRC and revenue: written & earned premium by line and province; exposure measures (policy counts, sums insured, risk units); reinsurance ceded premium & commissions; rate changes & mix of business.
> - Both must **reconcile** to P&C-1, which is the top-level aggregation of all IFRS 17 balances.
> - Exposure patterns in P&C-3 explain changes in P&C-2 development.
> - Reinsurance Held draws ceded claims balances from P&C-2 and ceded premium/commission balances from P&C-3.

## Part C — Application and Calculation
Source: [[Definition]], [[P&C]]

### C1. Building ultimate claims from components

An insurer's accident year 2023 position, as of December 31, 2025, is: cumulative claim payments $180 million; total case estimates $95 million; development on case estimates (IBNER) $12 million; IBNYR $45 million. Calculate reported claims, total IBNR, ultimate claims and the claim liability.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Reported claims = Cumulative claim payment + Case estimate = $180m + $95m = **$275m**
> - IBNR = IBNER + IBNYR = $12m + $45m = **$57m**
> - Ultimate Claims = Reported claims + IBNR = $275m + $57m = **$332m**
> - Check: Ultimate Claims = Cumulative claim payment + Case estimates + IBNER + IBNYR = $180m + $95m + $12m + $45m = $332m
> - Claim liability = Case estimates + IBNR = $95m + $57m = **$152m**
> - The ultimate should be reported with its specifications: time frame AY 2023, date used as of December 31, 2025.

### C2. Ultimate counts and average value of claims

For accident year 2023 a line has: closed counts 700, open counts 380, IBNR counts 120, and ultimate claims of $318 million. Calculate the ultimate counts, the average value of claims, and the proportion of the ultimate still not closed.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Ultimate counts = Closed counts + Open counts + IBNR counts = 700 + 380 + 120 = **1,200**
> - Average value of claims = Ultimate claims / Ultimate counts = $318,000,000 / 1,200 = **$265,000**
> - Not yet closed = Open counts + IBNR counts = 380 + 120 = 500, so $500 / 1,200 = $41.7\%$ of ultimate counts are not closed
> - Counts and average value of claims are themselves valid ultimate values, since an ultimate can include counts and the average value of claims.

### C3. Deriving ultimate claims from a loss ratio

For accident year 2023, ultimate earned premium is $500 million and the expected loss ratio is $62\%$. At December 31, 2025 cumulative paid claims are $160 million and case estimates are $120 million. Calculate ultimate claims, reported claims, total IBNR and the claim liability, and explain what the IBNR represents.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Ultimate claims = Ultimate earned premium $\times$ expected loss ratio = $500m \times 0.62 = **$310m**
> - Reported claims = Cumulative claim payment + Case estimate = $160m + $120m = **$280m**
> - IBNR = Ultimate Claims $-$ Reported claims = $310m $-$ $280m = **$30m**
> - The IBNR splits into IBNER (development on reported claims, including claims closed but that may reopen) plus IBNYR (pure IBNR for claims not yet reported).
> - Claim liability = Case estimates + IBNR = $120m + $30m = **$150m**
> - Expressing the ultimate through a ratio still requires the specifications: time frame AY 2023, date used as of December 31, 2025.

### C4. Rolling P&C-2 and P&C-3 into P&C-1

An insurer's P&C-2 unpaid claims analysis gives a best estimate of LIC of $420 million, a risk adjustment of $35 million and a discounting effect that reduces the liability by $18 million. P&C-3 feeds $260 million of premium and exposure into LRC. Insurance revenue for the period is built from coverage units of $150 million (P&C-3) plus claims emergence of $95 million (P&C-2). Reinsurance held comprises $30 million of ceded claims balances (P&C-2) and $12 million of ceded premium balances (P&C-3). Calculate the LIC, LRC, insurance revenue and reinsurance held reported in P&C-1.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - LIC = Best estimate + Risk adjustment + Discounting = $420m + $35m $-$ $18m = **$437m**
> - LRC = **$260m**, fed by premium + exposure from P&C-3
> - Insurance revenue = Coverage units (P&C-3) + Claims emergence (P&C-2) = $150m + $95m = **$245m**
> - Reinsurance Held = Ceded claims balances (P&C-2) + Ceded premium balances (P&C-3) = $30m + $12m = **$42m**
> - P&C-1 is the top-level aggregation and must reconcile to P&C-2 (claims) and P&C-3 (premium/exposure).

## Part D — Exam-Style Questions
Source: [[Definition]], [[Use of ultimate claims]], [[Materiality]], [[Subsequent events]], [[P&C]]

### D1. Ultimate claims and IFRS 17 financial reporting

Explain how ultimate claims estimates support an insurer's IFRS 17 financial reporting, including the role of the Appointed Actuary.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Liability values need to be estimated in financial reports to be in line with IFRS 17.
> - Ultimate Claims = Reported claims + IBNR, with IBNR = IBNER + IBNYR; Claim liability = Case estimates + IBNR.
> - Appointees Actuaries (AA) need to disclose their opinion on the adequacy of the reserve amount.
> - Incurred claims are needed to derive underwriting (UW) income, and ultimates help prepare financial statement disclosures.
> - Triangle-based reserving in P&C-2 supports the LIC reported in P&C-1; P&C-1 must reconcile to P&C-2 and P&C-3.
> - An ultimate must be specified by time frame (AY, CY, RY, PY) and date used, since the total cost of insurance is not yet known.

### D2. Compare incurred claims and ultimate claims

Compare incurred claims with ultimate claims and explain the practical consequences of using each.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - Incurred/reported claims = cumulative claim payment + case estimate, an interim measure at a point in time.
> - Ultimate claims add development on case estimates (IBNER) and IBNYR: Ultimate Claims = Reported claims + IBNR.
> - Case estimates change over time as more information is gathered, and IBNER includes provisions for claims closed but that may reopen.
> - IBNYR is pure IBNR, so ultimates capture claims not yet reported at all.
> - Ultimates are required for pricing past periods to set rate levels, for planning and budgeting and resource allocation, for employee bonuses, for financial condition analysis after an extreme event, and for M&A valuation.

### D3. P&C exhibits mapped to IFRS 17

Describe P&C-1, P&C-2 and P&C-3 and explain how they map to the IFRS 17 concepts of LIC, LRC, risk adjustment and insurance revenue.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **P&C-1** is the top-level aggregation: Statement of Financial Position (assets, liabilities including LIC and LRC), Statement of Profit or Loss (insurance revenue, insurance service result, insurance finance income/expense), Capital & Solvency exhibits (MCT components, available vs required capital) and the Reinsurance Held summary (gross, ceded, net). It must reconcile to P&C-2 and P&C-3.
> - **P&C-2** holds development triangles (paid, incurred, counts, ALAE / ULAE), unpaid claims analysis (best estimate, discounting, risk adjustment, reinsurance recoveries), large losses and catastrophe claims, and layered reinsurance impacts.
> - **P&C-3** holds written & earned premium by line and province, exposure measures (policy counts, sums insured, risk units), reinsurance ceded premium & commissions, and rate changes & mix of business.
> - **LIC** is reported in P&C-1 and is fed by best estimate + risk adjustment + discounting from P&C-2.
> - **LRC** is reported in P&C-1 and is fed by premium + exposure from P&C-3.
> - Risk adjustment is explicitly disclosed in P&C-1 and derived from uncertainty observed in P&C-2 (volatility, tail risk, cat exposure, reinsurance structure).
> - Insurance revenue is driven by coverage units (P&C-3) and claims emergence (P&C-2); exposure patterns in P&C-3 explain changes in P&C-2 development.
> - Reinsurance Held draws ceded claims balances from P&C-2 and ceded premium balances from P&C-3.

### D4. Materiality and subsequent events in a valuation report

You are preparing a valuation report and become aware of an event after the calculation date but before the report date. Explain how you set the materiality threshold for the work and how you handle the event.

> [!answer]- Answer
> - **Materiality:** an omission or misstatement is material if the judgment of a reasonable person would have been changed or influenced by its inclusion or correction, given surrounding circumstances; the user is key.
> - The threshold depends on the needs, skill, sophistication and experience of the intended audience, and is task-specific rather than product specific.
> - It should not change in time, and it varies with the size of the entity, access to capital, stage of the organizational life cycle, type of business (multi-line vs single line, personal vs commercial lines) and net retention.
> - Disclose materiality only where it will not create misunderstanding or confusion for the intended user, and define the purpose of the work adequately so it cannot be misused.
> - **Subsequent event:** an event first becoming known after the calculation date but before the corresponding report date; it must be material to be considered.
> - If it provides evidence of conditions existing at the end of the reporting period (or retroactively changes the entity at the calculation date), treat it as an **adjusting event** and include it in the calculations.
> - If it indicates conditions arising after the reporting period, treat it as a **non-adjusting event** and disclose it.
> - If the purpose of the work is to report on the entity as it will be as a result of the event, account for the event accordingly.
