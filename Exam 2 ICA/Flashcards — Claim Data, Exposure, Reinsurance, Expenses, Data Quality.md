
## Aggregation of Claim Data

### Calendar Year (CY)

**Q:** What is the definition of Calendar Year (CY) aggregation? **A:** All transactions occurring during the calendar year, regardless of policy effective date or accident date.

**Q:** Why is CY data not used for estimating unpaid claims? **A:** It does not align with the period of risk and mixes multiple accident periods.

### Accident Year (AY)

**Q:** What is Accident Year (AY) aggregation? **A:** All claims from accidents occurring in the same year, regardless of report date.

**Q:** What is the main disadvantage of AY aggregation? **A:** Earned premium mismatch with CY earned premium.

### Policy Year (PY)

**Q:** What is Policy Year (PY) aggregation? **A:** All claims from policies written during the calendar year.

**Q:** Why is PY aggregation slow to develop? **A:** It spans two calendar years.

### Report Year (RY)

**Q:** What is Report Year (RY) aggregation? **A:** All claims reported during the year.

**Q:** Why is RY not suitable for reserving? **A:** It does not capture IBNR.

## Claim Data at Alternate Limits

**Q:** Why do actuaries use alternate limits? **A:** To reduce volatility caused by large losses.

**Q:** What must be added after capping losses at an alternate limit? **A:** A large loss load.

**Q:** What must be documented when using alternate limits? **A:** Whether ALAE is included or excluded in the limit.

## Exposure Data

**Q:** What is Calendar Year exposure? **A:** All premium transactions during the year.

**Q:** What is Policy Year exposure? **A:** All policies written during the year.

**Q:** CY exposure is typically paired with which claim aggregations? **A:** AY and RY.

**Q:** PY exposure is paired with which claim aggregation? **A:** PY claims.

## Homogeneity & Statistical Reliability

**Q:** What is homogeneity in actuarial data grouping? **A:** Grouping claims with similar characteristics (severity, frequency, reporting patterns).

**Q:** What is statistical reliability? **A:** Ensuring enough claim volume for stable results.

**Q:** What happens if data is subdivided too much? **A:** Homogeneity increases but credibility decreases.

## Reinsurance Analysis

**Q:** What is the difference between gross and net of reinsurance? **A:** Gross includes assumed; net = direct + assumed − ceded.

**Q:** Why analyze claims gross and net? **A:** Gross for pricing and reinsurance negotiation; net for reserve adequacy.

**Q:** What factors influence reinsurance analysis? **A:** Program structure, data availability, volume, impact on claims, professional judgment.

## ULAE vs ALAE

**Q:** What is ULAE? **A:** General claim‑related expenses not tied to a specific claim.

**Q:** What is ALAE? **A:** Expenses directly tied to a specific claim.

**Q:** How is ULAE estimated? **A:** On an aggregate basis.

**Q:** How is ALAE estimated? **A:** Allocated per claim.

## Data Quality & Validation

**Q:** What are the two core requirements for actuarial data? **A:** Sufficiency and reliability.

**Q:** What must actuaries do when relying on data from others? **A:** Validate and disclose reliance.

**Q:** What is data reconciliation? **A:** Ensuring data accuracy and completeness.

**Q:** What must actuaries document regarding data quality? **A:** Validation steps, deficiencies, and impacts on results.

**Q:** Why must actuaries understand changes in insurer operations? **A:** Operational changes affect claim patterns and actuarial work.
