- ## Assumptions:

    - Claims count and severity will continue to develop in future periods as they have in the past.
    - Consistent definition of claim counts throughout the experience period.
    - The mix and types of claims are relatively homogeneous.
    - Better estimates of ultimate claims can be achieved by separating frequency and severity.
    - All chain ladder assumptions are also true
- ## Common Usage:

    - Immature experience periods.
    - Following the introduction of new GI products or territories when historical experience is limited.
    - When large changes make historical data unrepresentative of the future.
- ## Trends:

    - Used to adjust historical frequencies and severities from the cost level of the experience period to the cost level of the projected period.
- ## Advantages (When to Use):

    - Assumptions about inflation and expected claim disposal can be directly integrated into the methods.
    - Greater insight is gained into the claim process.
    - Potentially can derive a better estimate of ultimate claims
    - Help to better understand how the internal and external environment are influencing claims
- ## Disadvantages (When Not to Use):

    - Estimates are highly sensitive to trend estimates.
    - Changes in the definition of claim counts impact the estimates.
    - Changes in claim reporting or processing impact the estimates.
    - The method requires a relatively homogeneous mix of business.
    - The necessary data may not be available.
