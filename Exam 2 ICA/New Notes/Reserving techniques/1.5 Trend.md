## Goal

- The goal is to adjust historical claim experience to a cost level associated with a common point in time.
- Takes into account:
	- economic
	- societal
	- demographic forces
	- regulatory and legal

## How

- Look at trend in ultimate frequency, ultimate severity and ultimate pure premium.
- Exponential fit is better for decreasing trend than linear fit.

## Factors that will influence Frequency and Severity

- Deductible
	- Shift in average deductible will affect:
		- Freq (claims under deductible will not happen anymore)
		- Severity (increasing deductible = increase severity in general if heavy tail)
- Policy limits
	- Higher limit = higher severity and higher trend in severity
- Coverages
	- Depends on what happens
- Other characteristics affecting the mix of business in the portfolio

## Trending procedures

- Seek a balance between responsiveness and stability
	- Need a sufficient volume of credible experience from a long enough period of time
	- We want to trend premiums that are on level to take into account the changes in rate changes. We want the trends to only capture the effect of change in mix of exposures and inflation (if needed)

## Type of data used

- Same data as the one used to project ultimate claims
- Need ultimate projected values
- Need to balance stability (longer period of data) with responsiveness (shorter period of data)
- Short tail can assign more weight to recent years as there is more certainty around ultimate claim estimate (4 to 5 years)
- For long tail, short and long time (8 to 10 years) period is considered while calculating trend
- Might need to remove more immature years as the claim uncertainty is really high
- Might need to remove historical years if there were major changes that might affect the trends
- Might need to remove outliers
- Need to consider when selecting data points:
	- relevance of the historical experience
	- reliability of the recent data points
	- purpose for which the trend will be used
	- goodness of fit statistics from the regression analysis
- Data would need to be adjusted for the following:
	- Large claims
	- Large number of large claims
	- Catastrophe claims
	- Seasonality
	- Tort reform
	- Legislated benefits level changes

## Exposure Trending

- Why do we trend exposures?
	- If we work with inflation-sensitive exposure:
		- Types of exposures:
			- payroll
			- sales
			- total insured values
		- External economic indicators are used frequently to determine those trends
	- If there was a major change in mix of exposure:
		- change in limit or deductible
		- only account for those trends if we think they will continue in the future
- How do we figure out trends due to shift in mix of business?
	- Look at change in average quarterly on-level premium:
		- We want to remove distortion that could happen if the size of the book of business is changing
		- Need to be certain that inflation does not impact premium
			- Premium based on inflation-sensitive exposures
	- OR, look at average quarterly exposure changes:
		- No need to on-level premiums
		- Eliminates distortion in the average premium values that may be due to a change in a variety of rating factors

- Written premium:
	- Use average written date of written premium of each of the exposure periods to the average written date of forecast period
	- Preferred because they reflect shift in exposures more quickly than earned premiums
- Earned premium:
	- Use average earned date of earned premium of each of the exposure periods to the average earned date of forecast period
	- More widely used
