- Standards define trend consideration.
- Economic inflation is differentiated from social inflation.
- Actuary seek a balance between responsiveness and stability
- Need a credible amount of data for a long enough period of time
- Trend setting if judgement base and results can be different between actuaries 

## Trending Analysis

- Need to consider:
    - Economic inflation
    - Deductible leveraging (if the deductible is stable and there is inflation, the deductible will be less useful as it eliminates fewer claim dollars)
    - Utilization (some general insurance coverage may become more used)
    - Change in the mix of insureds or of coverages
    - Technology advances

## Good Practices

- General data good practices should apply when choosing data to calculate trend on.
- When selecting data, the actuary should consider:
    - The credibility assigned to the data by the actuary
    - The time period for which the data are available
    - The relationship to the items being trended
    - The effect of known biases or distortions on the data relied upon

## Variations in Trend

Trend can vary due to:

- Regional cost variations
- Design of insurance products
- Demographics

## How many data points to include

- Need to balance stability ( look at longer period of time) and responsiveness (look at most recent experience)
- Short tail line -> look at 4 to 5 year of exposure since the ultimate claim estimate is more certain
- long tail line -> look at short and long tail trend indication. More uncertainty around ultimate claim value. May not want to look at more recent point since they are highly leverage
- Need to remove outliers
- Need to consider:
	- The relevance of the historical experience to the period for which the trend is being determined;
	- The reliability of recent data points;
	-  The purpose for which the trend will be used; and
	- The goodness of fit statistics from the regression analysis.
## Linear VS Exponential analysis

- actuary would need to adjust historical data:
	- Unusually large claims;
	- An unusually large number of large claims;
	- Catastrophe claims;
	- Seasonality;
	- Changes resulting from tort and product reform; and/or
	- Legislated benefit-level changes.
- Linear = trend that increase or decrease by a constant amount
	- may not be appropriate for decreasing trend
- exponential = constant rate of change
- compare models with R squared or F statistics or P value
