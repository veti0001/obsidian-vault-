### 1. Insurer Management

Management uses ultimate claim estimates to:

- Determine whether reserves are adequate.
- Assess profitability of insurance portfolios.
- Make pricing and underwriting decisions.
- Monitor claim trends and emerging risks.

### 2. Investors and Shareholders

Investors are interested because ultimate claims directly affect:

- Reported profits.
- Future cash flows.
- The insurer's financial position.
- Company valuation.

If ultimate claims increase unexpectedly, profits usually decrease because additional liabilities must be recognized.

### 3. Regulators

Insurance regulators monitor ultimate claim estimates to:

- Ensure insurers remain solvent.
- Verify that policyholder obligations can be met.
- Assess capital adequacy and risk exposure.

### 4. Policyholders

Policyholders are concerned indirectly because:

- Adequate claim reserves increase confidence that future claims will be paid.
- Insurer solvency protects policyholder benefits.

### 5. Reinsurers

Reinsurers need estimates of ultimate claims to:

- Calculate amounts they may need to reimburse insurers.
- Assess profitability of reinsurance treaties.
- Establish their own reserves.

### 6. Rating Agencies

Organizations such as AM Best, Moody's, and S&P review ultimate loss estimates to:

- Evaluate reserve adequacy.
- Assess financial strength.
- Determine insurer credit ratings.

### 7. Auditors

Auditors examine ultimate claim estimates because:

- Insurance liabilities are often the largest liability on an insurer's balance sheet.
- The estimates involve significant judgement and assumptions.
- They must ensure financial statements are fairly presented.
