
## Overview

- Actuaries need to subdivide data into groups that exhibit similar characteristics.
- The goal is to find the right balance between **homogeneity** and **statistical reliability**.
- The most common split for data groups is by line of business.

## Key Concepts

### Homogeneity

- **Definition:** Groups should exhibit similar characteristics, including:
    - Reporting and settlement patterns
    - Claim severity and frequency
    - Relationship between claim-related expenses and indemnity
    - Propensity for large claims

### Statistical Reliability (Credibility)

- **Definition:** Ensuring there is a sufficient volume of homogeneous claims in each group to produce statistically stable and meaningful results.

## The Balancing Act

- Subdividing data too much increases **homogeneity** but decreases **statistical reliability** due to smaller sample sizes.
- Conversely, keeping groups too broad increases **reliability** but sacrifices **homogeneity**.
- Actuaries must carefully balance these two competing needs during ratemaking, reserving, and data analysis.
