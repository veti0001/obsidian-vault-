-  Number: 1460
- Definition:
	- The actuary should implement appropriate quality assurance processes prior to the release of work to users.
- Other useful info:
	- Subsection 1460 applies to quality assurance processes that are at the instigation of the actuary responsible for the work: quality control in the actuary's firm or employer as well as review by persons external to the actuary's firm or employer.
	- In deciding what processes are appropriate and proportionate, whether different processes suit different elements of the work, and when the processes would be carried out, consider: the degree of difficulty, the extent of professional judgment required, and the overall complexity of the work; the purpose of the work and the extent to which users may reasonably be expected to challenge it; the significance of the work, including financial, reputational, or other consequences for users; the reasonable expectations of users; whether the way the work is carried out makes it vulnerable to errors; the novelty of the work and the actuary's experience in similar engagements; and whether there are legislative or regulatory requirements for the work to be peer reviewed.
	- Processes include calculation control procedures and model validation (see [[Control]]), calculation result examination (see [[Reasonableness of results]]), self-checking of the work, repetition of the work, and peer review. Appropriate processes may differ for different elements of the work.
	- Peer review: a process by which one or more components of an actuary's work are considered by at least one other individual to provide assurance as to the quality of the work; it can be an important component of quality assurance.
	- The actuary should select a peer reviewer with appropriate experience and expertise. If a person is qualified to have performed the work to be reviewed, that is prima facie evidence the person is also qualified to perform the peer review.
	- Consider the extent to which peer review should be independent: a review by at least one individual not otherwise involved in the work, with appropriate experience and expertise, and in a position to effectively challenge the work. The perceived objectivity of a reviewer is enhanced by independence from the actuary performing the work.
	- Where individuals are involved in the quality assurance processes, clarify each person's role and responsibilities.
	- For actuarial evidence work, peer review might be precluded by the circumstances affecting the work; its absence in such circumstances does not indicate a weakness in the quality assurance processes. Where the actuary is expected or required to be independent in performing the work, define the scope of any peer review so as not to impair such independence.
