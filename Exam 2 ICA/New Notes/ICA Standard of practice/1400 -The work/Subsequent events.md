-  Number: 1430
- Definition:
	- The actuary should correct any data defect or calculation error that is revealed by a subsequent event.
- Other useful info:
	- For work with respect to an entity, the actuary should not take a subsequent event into account (other than in a pro forma calculation) unless the subsequent event:
		- Provides information about the entity as it was at the calculation date;
		- Retroactively makes the entity different at the calculation date; or
		- Makes the entity different after the calculation date and a purpose of the work is to report on the entity as it will be as a result of the event.
	- The actuary should report a subsequent event that is not taken into account in the work.
	- Classification: a subsequent event is relevant if it reveals an error, provides information about the entity, or is a decision that makes the entity different. Events other than those revealing errors are classified, and the actuary either takes the event into account or reports but does not take it into account.
	- Entities: the pension plan (valuation of a pension plan); the block of annuity business (insurance contract liabilities for an issuer's annuity business); the pension plan combined with the member's specific data (a member's individual entitlement); the insurance company (valuing insurance contract liabilities).
	- Events providing information about the entity as it was at the calculation date: publication of an experience study useful for selecting assumptions; reporting of a claim incurred on or before the calculation date; adoption of a pension plan amendment prior to the calculation date of which the actuary becomes aware after it.
	- Events retroactively making the entity different at the calculation date: definitive or virtually definitive decisions made after the calculation date but effective on or before it, such as winding up a pension plan (partially or fully); selling a portion of a participating employer's business and spinning off its members; amending the benefits of a pension plan; transferring a portion of an insurer's policies to another insurer; or invoking a judicial decision that nullifies or significantly modifies the law affecting insurance claims.
	- If the event provides information about the entity as it was or retroactively makes it different at the calculation date, the effect is the same as if the actuary first became aware of it on or before the calculation date: the actuary would not report the event as a subsequent event.
	- If the event makes the entity different after the calculation date and the purpose is to report on the entity as it will be as a result of the event: take the event into account and describe it, and establish a cut-off date as close to the report date as practicable after which events are not reflected (the cut-off date may vary by assumption).
	- If the purpose is to report on the entity as it was at the calculation date: do not take the event into account, but report it since it affects the entity's future operations and subsequent calculations.
	- Classification may differ depending on the circumstances affecting the work:
		- Precipitous stock market fall: not reflected when valuing an insurer's assets or liabilities for financial statements, but reflected (subject to the cut-off date) when assessing the viability of an entity (e.g. a social insurance program) over the projection period.
		- Salary freeze: if it corrects excessive salaries, it provides additional information about the entity as it was at the calculation date; if it deals with a recent problem, it indicates a change in conditions that makes the entity different after the calculation date. Consider whether the effect is lasting or will be compensated by higher salaries later.
		- Default on a bond or insolvency of a reinsurer: if it was the culmination of a gradual deterioration, most of which occurred before the calculation date, it provides additional information about the entity as it was; if precipitated by a catastrophe, it makes the entity different after the calculation date.
	- Reporting: the actuary may report, as an alternative, the opposite calculation (one that does not take the subsequent event into account when the main calculation does, or vice versa). Example: a pension valuation following marriage breakdown with the calculation date at separation may consider reporting values assuming an early retirement subsequent event had been an established intention at the calculation date. The calculations would be the same regardless of the purpose of the work, but the reporting would depend on the purpose.
