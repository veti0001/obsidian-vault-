-  Number: 1520
- Definition:
	- The actuary should cooperate with an auditor who wishes to use the actuary’s work in accordance with the following Joint Policy Statement.
	- In this subsection, “post-employment benefit plans” means a non-pension employee future benefit plan.
- Other useful info:
	- The Joint Policy Statement concerns communications between actuaries involved in the preparation of financial statements and auditors; approved by the Canadian Actuarial Standards Board and the Auditing and Assurance Standards Board; effective for communications initiated on or after March 31, 2023 (early application permitted).
	- Purpose: facilitate effective communication between the two professionals; sets out (a) communications about their respective responsibilities, (b) how they interact in carrying them out, and (c) how responsibilities may be disclosed to readers of the financial statements.
	- Applies when: (a) the auditor audits financial statements prepared in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards that include amounts determined by or with the assistance of an actuary; or (b) the actuary uses the auditor’s work in the actuarial valuation to determine amounts in the financial statements. Examples: pension plan, post-employment benefit plan (and sponsor) and insurance enterprise financial statements.
	- Not intended for requests to do work outside the audit or outside the actuarial valuation — consider recommending another engagement type (e.g., CSRS 4400 Agreed-Upon Procedures).
	- Does not apply to communications between an auditor and the auditor’s actuary, or between an actuary and the external review actuary; does not supplant professional responsibilities under applicable professional standards.
	- Key terms:
		- Inquiring professional: the actuary or auditor using the work of the other; responding professional: the one whose work is being used.
		- Underlying engagement: the financial statement audit for the auditor; the engagement to determine, or assist management in determining, actuarial amounts in the financial statements for the actuary.
		- Actuary involved in the preparation of financial statements: an employee of the company or an external consultant who determines and reports on amounts in the financial statements.
		- Auditor’s actuary: an actuary with the necessary skills, knowledge and experience used by the auditor to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence.
		- External review actuary: reviews another actuary’s work at the request of a regulator and provides an opinion on whether it meets applicable professional standards and accepted actuarial practice.
		- Applicable professional standards: for actuaries, the CIA Standards of Practice and Rules of Professional Conduct; for auditors, Canadian Standards on Quality Management, Canadian Auditing Standards, and relevant ethical/independence requirements.
		- Data includes invested assets and membership of a pension plan or post-employment benefit plan, policies of and claims against an insurance enterprise, and reinsurance of an insurance enterprise.
	- Responsibilities: the financial statements are the responsibility of management; the auditor is responsible for an opinion on whether they are prepared, in all material respects, per the applicable financial reporting framework.
	- Using the other’s work starts with the inquiring professional initiating a discussion with the responding professional (e.g., the actuary may discuss the auditor’s procedures on data integrity; the auditor may discuss the actuary’s assumptions, methods and data).
	- The inquiring professional may use the responding professional’s work provided they take reasonable care to determine there is a basis for such use. Use does not constitute reliance: the actuary remains responsible for their work, and the auditor retains sole responsibility for the audit opinion.
	- Communication would be established when planning each engagement and continue as necessary; each professional seeks from management the right to communicate with the other and to disclose relevant information when necessary.
	- The inquiring professional would: inform the responding professional of the intended use (including of their work or name in third-party communications, to avoid inappropriate use); request confirmation of the underlying engagement, good standing, and performance per applicable professional standards; and make the responding professional aware of needs: the financial reporting framework and accounting policy choices vs actuarial valuation choices, the application of [[Materiality]], [[Subsequent events]] treatment, timing of the work and report date, and any questions.
	- The responding professional would provide a written response confirming: availability to perform the work; the underlying engagement; good standing; qualification (including required certifications or designations); performance per applicable professional standards; awareness of the intended use (including third-party use); and any problems expected in meeting the inquiring professional’s needs on a timely basis.
	- Qualifications: for an auditor, prima facie professional qualification is membership in good standing in a professional accounting body; for an actuary, fellowship in good standing in the CIA. If not well known, the inquiring professional may consult others familiar with the responding professional’s reputation for competence and integrity.
	- The post-completion written response would: identify the relationship to the entity; identify the financial statements or data to which the work relates; describe the work in the context of the underlying engagement and the request; for auditors, state that the work is not an assurance engagement (hence no opinion on the data); require prior written consent before quoting, referring to, or providing the work to others; and, when appropriate, include a copy of the report on the underlying engagement.
	- Questions about an aspect of the responding professional’s work would be raised with them for a reasonable explanation; this does not restrict the inquiring professional from obtaining information required in performing their duties.
	- When required by law or regulation, a description of the respective responsibilities of the auditor and the actuary would accompany the financial statements.
