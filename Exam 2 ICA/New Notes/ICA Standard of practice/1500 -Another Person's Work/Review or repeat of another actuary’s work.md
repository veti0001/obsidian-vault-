-  Number: 1530
- Definition:
	- The standards in this subsection apply to a review engagement that is at the instigation of a user; they do not apply to quality control in the first actuary’s firm or employer, even if the reviewer is external. They also apply, mutatis mutandis, to a repeat engagement.
	- If the terms of the first actuary’s engagement so permit, then the first actuary should cooperate with the reviewer.
- Other useful info:
	- Terms: “first actuary” = actuary whose work is reviewed or repeated; “review engagement” = engagement to review the first actuary’s work; “reviewer” = actuary engaged to review or repeat the work; “repeat engagement” = engagement to repeat all or part of the first actuary’s work.
	- Discussion: where the terms of the review engagement so permit, the reviewer should, as soon as practical, discuss the review with the first actuary (unless the reviewer’s agreement with the work makes such discussion superfluous), attempt to resolve any difference, and report the result.
	- The reviewer should report if: the work is within the range of accepted actuarial practice despite a reported disagreement; a limitation in time, information, data, or resources constrained the quality of the work; discussion resulted in improvement to the work (or, for periodic reporting, to the subsequent report); or the work is not within the range of accepted actuarial practice.
	- Selection of reviewer: may be selected by a user of the first actuary’s work or by the first actuary. Selection by the first actuary would not be appropriate if it creates a potential conflict of interest (e.g., where the interests of the user and the first actuary’s client or employer are opposed), but may otherwise be appropriate to facilitate compliance with this subsection and to help assure selection of a qualified reviewer. The first actuary would take into account the user’s objective for the review and consult with the user as appropriate.
	- An actuary qualified to perform the first actuary’s work is prima facie qualified to be the reviewer; the perceived objectivity of the reviewer is enhanced if the reviewer is independent of the first actuary.
	- Timing: a pre-release review (before the first actuary’s report is released) gives the reviewer the opportunity to suggest improvement; a post-release review allows improvement only in future work and may require withdrawal of the report and revision of the work.
	- Differences: the reviewer would report a material difference along with an explanation of the reason. A non-material difference would not be reported if it would lead to an unnecessary dispute with the first actuary, but the reviewer would report if they had access to different data, information, or resources, or different time constraints, than the first actuary. The reviewer would also report if access to different data, information or resources would reduce uncertainty in the interpretation of the work.
	- Appropriate review engagement: would consider the appropriateness of an engagement precluding discussion with the first actuary (especially if the first actuary will not be apprised of the review); it may be appropriate where the interests of the respective clients/employers are opposed (especially actuarial evidence work in litigation or mediation), where a judicial, legal or regulatory authority is investigating the first actuary’s conduct, or where the review is merely preliminary to a fuller review with open discussion. Limiting or delaying discussion may be appropriate where the reviewer’s client or employer wants the two reports to be independent. In evidence work, the reviewer may report without discussion results based on assumptions differing from the first actuary’s, or alternatives within the range of accepted actuarial practice.
	- Repeat engagement: appropriate if its purpose is to identify or reduce uncertainty in the interpretation of the first actuary’s work. If the second actuary knows or suspects the engagement is a repeat, they would consider the possibility of “opinion shopping” by the client or employer; such an engagement may not be appropriate.
