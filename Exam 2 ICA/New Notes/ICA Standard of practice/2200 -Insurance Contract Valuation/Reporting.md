-  Number: 2230
- Definition:
	- The actuary’s report — a summary report as described in subsection 1740 ([[Summary report]]) — should: conform to relevant Canadian federal and provincial legislation that requires the actuary to value the policy liabilities, not only the insurance contract liabilities; describe the valuation and presentation of policy liabilities for the insurer’s financial statements prepared in accordance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS); include the actuary’s opinion on the appropriateness of those policy liabilities and on the fairness of their presentation; and describe the actuary’s role in the preparation of the insurer’s financial statements if that role is not described in those statements or their accompanying management discussion and analysis. (Effective January 1, 2023.)
- Other useful info:
	- 2230.02 Standard reporting language: if the actuary can report without reservation, the report should consist of a scope paragraph (describes the actuary’s work) and an opinion paragraph (favourable opinion on the valuation and its presentation); otherwise the actuary should modify the standard reporting language to report with reservation. Square-bracketed wording is variable and may be adjusted for interim statements and terminology.
	- Presentation of policy liabilities (2230.03–.04): valuation for IFRS financial statements requires valuing items beyond the total policy liabilities; all items derived from that valuation reported in the statement of financial position, statement of financial performance, statement of changes in equity, statement of cash flows and the notes (disclosures) are part of the presentation. Examples of such items: the change in the liability for remaining coverage presented as insurance revenue; calculation and projection of coverage units used to allocate release of the contractual service margin over current and future reporting periods; identification of the components (present value of future cash flows, risk adjustment for non-financial risk, contractual service margin) for each of — portfolios of insurance contracts issued that are assets/liabilities and portfolios of reinsurance contracts held that are assets/liabilities; and reconciliation of the change in the contractual service margin or loss component.
	- Actuary’s role (2230.05–.08): the insurer is responsible for the reported information — identification, combination, aggregation, separation, recognition and derecognition of contracts, the choice of measurement approach and assumptions, the measurement calculations and the disclosures. Where legislation requires, the actuary is responsible for valuing policy liabilities and reporting to policyholders and shareholders, so the summary report should include a description of that role if the statements or MD&A omit it. Illustrative wording is provided (the Appointed Actuary ensures the valuation accords with accepted actuarial practice in Canada, applicable legislation and regulations/directives, and opines on the appropriateness of the policy liabilities and the fairness of their presentation — framed as policy liabilities, not just insurance contract liabilities). MD&A may also describe the actuary’s formal responsibilities beyond statement preparation, e.g. annual financial condition testing and reporting to the directors ([[Report to the directors]]).
	- Alignment with the auditor’s report (2230.11–.12): uniformity of common features avoids confusion — addressees (policyholders of a mutual insurer; participating policyholders and shareholders of a stock insurer), years referenced (usually only the current year), and report date (same date entails taking account of the same [[Subsequent events]]). Communication with the auditor is desirable at various stages covering: mutual use of each other’s work; drafting of common features; drafting a report with reservation; presentation of insurance contract and other policy liabilities and other items valued by the actuary; and treatment of subsequent events ([[Communication with the auditor]]).
	- Unusual situations (2230.13–.15): the report signals to the reader whether presentation is fair. If fair presentation requires explanation, the notes to the financial statements should provide it (including, where appropriate, the effect on the statements); in their absence the actuary provides it through a reservation in reporting. Helpful test: "Will explanation enhance the user’s understanding of the insurer’s financial statements?" Examples of unusual situations: capital appropriated or repatriated on the actuary’s advice; off-balance-sheet obligations (e.g., contingent policy liabilities in connection with market conduct); restatement of prior-period items; inconsistency among financial reporting periods; impracticality of restating; an unusual relationship between current items and expected future items; a methodology change with no current effect but expected future effect; a difference between the insurer’s practices (e.g., policy for setting dividend scales) and those the actuary assumed; and a subsequent event ([[Materiality]]).
	- Consistency across reporting periods (2230.16–.18): meaningful comparability requires consistency, achieved by restating prior-period items previously reported on an inconsistent basis; disclosure of the inconsistency is a less desirable alternative. A change in valuation methodology may create inconsistency; a change in assumptions reflecting a changed expected outlook does NOT (though if material, fair presentation requires disclosure). A change in assumptions resulting from the application of new standards may create an inconsistency.
	- Reservations in reporting (2230.19–.29): "reference" wording points to the paragraph in the report that provides the additional explanation needed for fair presentation. Modified wording is illustrated for: a new appointment using the predecessor actuary’s work without taking responsibility for it (doubts about that work after review → a more serious reservation); the impracticality of restating (e.g., a change in the methodology for measuring coverage units used to allocate and recognize the contractual service margin); a takeover of an insurer whose records did not provide sufficient and reliable data, creating an unusual degree of uncertainty; financial statements reporting policy liabilities materially different from those the actuary calculated and reported (disclose the difference in amounts and identify where the explanation is found, including important reasons where possible); and a material change in assumption or methodology not disclosed in the statements (e.g., the methodology for measuring the risk adjustment) — modify the opinion paragraph to disclose.
	- No reservation required (2230.30): where the actuary uses an assumption or methodology set by another party but takes responsibility for the work, the opinion paragraph is not modified. Examples: the CIO selects different reference portfolios for groups of contracts with the same liquidity characteristics — both reasonably representative as IFRS 17 requires — even though the actuary sees an unnecessary inconsistency; the risk adjustment reflects the insurer’s requirements on longevity risk even though the actuary believes more compensation is warranted; and the CFO classifies expenses as "directly attributable" (material impact) where the actuary would not — in each case the actuary has confidence in the person’s qualifications, competence, integrity and objectivity and the view is reasonable.
	- The IFRS 17 risk adjustment for non-financial risk is NOT a provision for adverse deviations (2210.04; [[Provision for adverse deviations]]).
